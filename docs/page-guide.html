Page guide

How to read the two daily pages: MARKET-1's and the paper experiment's. Both pages are written by frozen programs, so they never change while their tests run; this guide sits beside them and changes nothing they show. Written 2026-11-16 by the supervisor, from the tests' rules (apps/poller/market1/rules.md and apps/poller/experiment/rules.md).

Where the pages are and when they change

Each page has a second file beside it, positions.html, with every position; the main page shows the newest 500. MARKET-1 reads the pool's trades that the paper experiment stored at 04:00Z.

Reading the numbers

Basis points (bps)

Every result is in basis points of the $1,000 each paper position puts in: 100 bps = 1% = $10. So −106.7 bps is −1.07%, about −$10.67, and +552.1 bps is +5.52%, about +$55.21. Results are after fees and funding unless a column says otherwise.

"−154.0 ± 64.4": an average and its uncertainty

The first number is the average result a position. The second is its standard error: how far that average could be from the true one by chance. It is computed so that one trader's many positions, or one busy day, cannot make it look more certain than it is. As a rule of thumb, the true average is very likely within two of these either side: here about −283 to −25.

The band at the next look

The average ± the next look's multiplier × its standard error: 2.81 at the first look (2026-12-19), 2.43 at the second (2027-02-06), 2.17 at the third (2027-05-08). Example: −154.0 ± 2.81 × 64.4 gives −334.9 … +26.9. Only the band at a look decides; a band that crosses a line between looks decides nothing.

The looks and the verdicts

  • KEEP: the whole band is above 0 and a second check agrees (the paper experiment: the copies beat random trades; MARKET-1: the result beats the coin's own usual drift).
  • DROP: the whole band is below the line the owner set: +10 bps for the paper experiment, +50 bps for MARKET-1.
  • WITHHELD: too many positions could not be priced properly (more than 2% for the paper experiment, 10% for MARKET-1).
  • TOO FEW TO TELL: none of these holds yet (MARKET-1 also needs at least 20 days and 10 markets).

The first KEEP or DROP is the test's answer. Until the first look the verdict box says "Too early to judge — next look 2026-12-19." Keep and drop are verdicts on a test, never instructions to trade.

Why the pages have no red and green

Both tests' rules bar colours for a result's sign, and MARKET-1's page hides the side lines' results until the looks. A running number must not pull at a test before it is judged. Colours for a position's state (pending, open, closed) carry no result and are planned for v1's own pages.

MARKET-1's page

MARKET-1 is the live paper test of the liquidation wave on Polymarket Perps: when two or more of the pool's accounts are forced to close positions on the same side in small coins within an hour, a paper position goes the other way 2 minutes later and is held 24 hours. Its sister test, WAVE-H, checks the same rule once on Hyperliquid's history and has no daily page.

"WAVE, complete weeks (positions before …)"

The headline counts only complete weeks: weeks for which every one of the pool's 1,602 accounts has been read. The paper experiment reads about a seventh of the accounts each day and the rest on Monday, so a week becomes complete after its Monday read.

"The pool's liquidations in small coins, by week"

For each week (its Monday): the pool's forced-close fills in small coins, how many different accounts were liquidated, and how many accounts traded small coins at all. The current week is low only because it is still partial.

The statuses in "net, bps"
  • a plain number (−106.7): final. Closed, both prices set.
  • pending: closed, but its prices wait for the next day's recordings to be sealed; the next run fills it in.
  • "(open, at the mark)": still open at the run's data end; a temporary value at the market's mark price that will change.

The paper experiment's page

The paper experiment copies, on paper, the new positions of a fixed pool of traders: each copy opens 62–95 seconds after the trader (a fixed delay per position, like a real follower's), puts in $1,000 and leaves when the trader leaves, step by step.

Line Whose positions it copies

Positions / Traders / Days / Open paper copies, the traders behind them, the days with a copy, and the copies still open

Band at the next look the range the next look will judge
Against random trades, bps each copy's 24-hour move, before costs, minus that of a random public trade in the same market and hour with the same delay: do these traders pick better moments than chance? The number in brackets is how many copies have had their full 24 hours.
Its band the band at the next look, for that number
Daily e-value each UTC day casts one vote: did that day's copies beat random trades? The e-value says how one-sided the votes are, in either direction. About 1 or below: mixed (3 of 7 days gives 0.46). 16.00 over 7 days: all 7 days voted the same way; by chance alone a value of 16 or more comes at most 1 time in 16. Read its direction from "against random trades". It decides nothing.

"Traders of the main line, by address"

One row per main-line trader: positions, open ones, the average result, and a 95% band shown once the trader has 20 days with positions ("too few days" before). About 1 in 20 such bands looks significant by chance alone, so these rows decide nothing.

The words in "Exits"
  • reduce: the trader closed part of the position; the copy closed the same share.
  • close: the trader closed the rest; so did the copy.
  • flip: the trader reversed the position.
  • A liquidation of the trader shows as reduce (in part) or close (in full).
  • marked: still open; the open part is valued at the mark price at the run's data end.
Traders leave differently: one closes at once ("close"), another trims in six steps ("reduce" six times, then "close"). Every step is copied, so the number of words varies. A compact form ("reduce ×6, close") is planned for v1's own pages.
