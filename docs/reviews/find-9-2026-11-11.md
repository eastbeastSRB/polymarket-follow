## For the owner, in one page

**The question.** When three or more traders with no visible link between
them open the same side of one market within an hour, and no directional
trader opens the other side in that time, does the price then move their way
over the next day by more than it does after random trades in the same market
and hour? If it did, such "agreement" would be a signal worth watching.

**The answer: no.** On the 138 such moments from 2026-08-31 to the cut among
the traders of the all-time volume board (130 of them in markets a taker can
trade at a day's horizon — 101 on BTC), the price moved, on average, **slightly
against the agreeing side, not with it**: −0.07 of a typical day's move by the
day average, +0.02 by the trader average — both indistinguishable from
nothing (the deciding p-value 0.87). In basis points: −20 by the day average,
−13 by the plain average of the moments. **Not passed.**

**[Final review, FIND-REVIEW-B, F8: what this "no" can and cannot say.** With
130 moments the measurement is coarse: its standard error is 0.153 of a
day's move (21 bps), so by the rule book's own yardstick (A.0-13, k 2.85)
this FIND could have seen only an effect of about 0.44 of a day's move —
about 60 bps over the day [arithmetic on the look's standard errors]. The
range the data leave open (95%) is −0.38 to +0.23 of a day's move by the
day average (−63 to +22 bps) and −0.22 to +0.26 by the trader average. So:
no sign of an effect, and a large one is ruled out; an edge of 10–20 bps a
day could be neither seen nor excluded here.**]**

**What a follower would have got.** Acting 62–96 seconds after the third trader
and holding a day, as a taker: about −21 bps a moment on average (−32 by the
day average); as a maker −15. Not significant either way — and no gain.

**Every check says the same:**
- *Random moments* in the same markets with the same directions: 137 of 200
  random sets did at least as well as the real moments.
- *The moments moved a day earlier or later:* nothing.
- *Without any one trader, market or day:* the outcome never changes.
- *All 1,702 traders* (including those who reached us through profit boards):
  +0.06 of a day's move, the deciding p 0.75 — the same nothing.
- *At six hours* (the entry's first horizon): −0.08, nothing.

**One side observation (decides nothing):** these moments tend to come after
the price had moved *against* the agreeing side in the hour before (−9 bps on
the day average) — buying after a dip rather than "a herd after news". It is
weak (by trader, p 0.37) and is a description, not a finding.

**What it means for round 2.** Entry 9 is **not sent to the exams** (it does
not enter the register; m stays one lower). Even had it passed, the exams
could only have seen an effect of about 77–104 bps a day — several times what
any print here shows.

*This is research on past data. It is not advice on what to trade.*

  after the third entry (entry 5's ground), or days.
- Whether it holds among accounts this study cannot see as linked (one owner,
  several wallets) or among non-directional accounts (makers, algorithms).
- Whether a stricter agreement — more accounts, larger size, a shorter window
  — behaves differently: not tested; the definition was fixed first.
- The owner's own cost setting (not defined yet).
- **A candidate for a later batch, not a finding:** these moments came after
  the price moved against the agreeing side in the hour before (−9.4 bps on
  the day average, p 0.045 by day, 0.37 by trader). "Agreement after a dip" is
  a description of when these traders act, not a test of what follows.
