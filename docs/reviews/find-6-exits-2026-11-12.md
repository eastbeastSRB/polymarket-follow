## For the owner, in one page

*Words.* A **copy** is a follower's copy of one new position of a trader, taken
about 80 seconds late (R8's delay). A **bp** is 0.01% of the copy's size.
"±" is one standard error, counted trader by trader.

**What "copying the exits" means.** FIND-6 held every copy for exactly one day.
Here every copy instead does what the trader does, after the same delay:
- when the trader closes, the copy closes;
- when the trader sells part of the position, the copy sells the same share
  of what it still holds;
- when the trader flips to the other side, the copy closes;
- when the exchange liquidates part of the trader's position, the copy is cut
  by the same share;
- adding to the position is not copied.

Every copy pays a taker's fees on both legs, the spread and depth at each
moment, and funding on what is still open. A copy still open on 7 November
(the end of our data) is valued at the last price before it.

**What it shows, in plain words.**
- **Every group still loses money with the exits copied.** Copying every
  candidate lost about 16 bps a copy, against 13 held a day; the difference is
  well within chance (−2.9 ± 9.5).
- **The traders leave fast.** Half of the copies were out within 47 minutes,
  and 44% within 30 minutes. A copy pays a round trip of about 10.4 bps for a
  short move: before costs the copies lost only 5.4 bps.
- **Your filter's top fifth lost much less with exits copied (−24 against −53),
  but still more than its rest (−14).** Its gap to the rest shrank from about
  −50 to −10 bps a copy, within chance. Much of that improvement is one
  account, `0xc58a5ade…` (the one whose afternoon of 4 November FIND-REVIEW-6
  named). Without it, the top fifth's gain from copying exits falls from +29 to
  +13 ± 19 bps a copy.
- **Exits did not flatter the win share here; they lowered it** (39% against
  48.5%), because many short copies end a few bps down after costs. The
  average win against the average loss rose (1.12 against 0.96) while the money
  got slightly worse. This is R3's warning: exits can flatter these two
  numbers, but never the money.
- **The 20 copies the traders were still holding on 7 November averaged
  −575 bps.** They are 1% of the copies but about 6 of every candidate's
  16 bps loss; the copies that closed lost 9.9 bps.

## 1. The rules and their hashes

**The rules** are decisions 2026-11-12, "an exploring check: FIND-6's copies
with the trader's exits copied", points (1)–(6), as corrected by "the exploring
check's Q1" (commit `283cd27`).

**(1) The copies:**
- FIND-6's deciding line: the rows of `look-entries.jsonl.gz` whose (window,
  account) is a board candidate in `look-cands.jsonl.gz` and whose dRU is not
  null;
- 1,918 copies of 106 traders (the supervisor's precision, 08:20Z);
- 33 more board rows have dRU null and no netBps: they are left out and
  counted;
- each copy keeps its own `id`, `t`, `dir`, `iid` and `D`, and is entered at
  t + D at the kit's `leg`, taker setting.

**(2) The exits** (R8's mirrored money):
- each exit is rebuilt from the raw fills: `readAccount` → `toEvents` →
  `buildEntries(events, −∞, ∞)`, matched by `entryId`;
- each REDUCE, CLOSE, FLIP or forced exit closes its share of what is still
  open of the copy, at the exit's first fill + D;
- **a partial liquidation closes its share** (Q1: the decisions' earlier "or
  liquidation" was a slip; R8's rule is the source);
- adds are not copied; a re-open merged inside 60 s does not re-open the copy;
- the copy's result is Σ partᵢ × `leg(mk, { dir, tIn: t + D, h: tᵢ − (t + D),
  setting: taker })`, where tᵢ is the exit instant (the exit's first fill + D);
  the code computes h as the exit's first fill − t, the same number without
  rounding.

**(3) The cut:**
- a copy still open at 2026-11-07T00:00Z, or whose exit instant falls at or
  after it, is marked at the cut and flagged;
- its last leg ends at **CUT − 1 ms**, in the panel's last minute,
  **2026-11-06T23:59:00–23:59:59.999Z**;
- that minute's mark is the 1-minute bucket that ends at the cut: the panel
  asked `mark-history` for marks up to exactly the cut, which cannot return a
  bucket that starts there (RESEARCH-KIT, section 1);
- the spread at that instant is the off-hours one (Friday 18:59 in New York);
- funding is charged only for hours in (tIn, CUT − 1 ms], so the 00:00Z
  settlement of 7 November is never read;
- the kit's readers refuse any fill, funding row or panel row at or after the
  cut;
- all 69 markets of the copies end at the cut and serve a mark in that minute;
- an unpriced copy is counted by its reason, never guessed (there were none).

**(4) The groups:** each rule's top fifth and its rest per window, exactly
`result.json` → `board.tops`, and every candidate alike. A trader in the top
fifth in one window and in the rest in the other is in both groups.

**(5) The prints:** section 3.

**Calls fixed before any outcome** (accepted in "the exploring check's Q1"):
- **the hold:** each copy's share-weighted mean hold, Σ partᵢ × hᵢ (median and
  quartiles); the time to its last leg is printed beside;
- **a gap copy:** a chain break on the instrument after the entry's first event
  and up to the last exit the copy used, or to the end of the data while the
  trader is still open. A chain break is an order event whose previous size is
  not the size the event before it left (relative tolerance 1e-9);
- **top minus the rest, each copy alike, with CR1:** the coefficient of a top
  indicator in a regression clustered by trader, with the factor G/(G − 1) and
  t on G − 1 degrees of freedom; each trader alike: Welch's test on the
  traders' means, as FIND-6's `netDifference`;
- **the biggest movers:** ranked by the absolute sum of (exits copied − 24 h)
  over each trader's copies;
- **one or two accounts:** a group's mean without the account whose removal
  moves it most, then without the next.

**The line beside, from Q1:** every liquidation fill closes the whole copy. A
liquidation exit is a forced exit whose order event holds a liquidation fill.
All 130 forced exits on the copies are liquidations (0 adl, 0 settlement).

## 4. What could fool us

- **The cut's marked copies.** 20 copies (1%) carry about 6 bps of every
  candidate's 15.8 bps loss. They are positions the traders kept open, nearly
  all long, valued at the 23:59Z mark of 6 November in weeks that fell for
  longs (FIND-REVIEW-6: long copies lost 74 bps gross). Their true outcome lies
  after the cut and is unknown. The closed copies alone lost 9.9 ± 4.6 bps
  (24 h: −11.4).
- **Short holds and minute marks.** 19% of copies were held under 5 minutes
  and 4% under 1 minute. The kit prices by the minute's end mark: a copy whose
  entry and exit fall in one minute gets a gross of 0 and pays its costs. On
  such holds the minute convention may mis-state the gross either way, though
  never the costs.
- **D = 0 is an instant follower at the minute's end, not at the trader's own
  fill price.** Its mark is on average about 30 s after the trader's fill,
  against about 110 s for the delayed follower (80 s, then the rest of its
  minute). So "the delay costs nothing" holds only at this resolution.
- **The gaps.** None of the 44 chain breaks in these accounts lies inside a
  copy's chain. Gaps cannot explain anything here.
- **One account.** `0xc58a5ade…` carries most of the improvement from copying
  exits in rule 7's top fifth, and all of it in rules 1, 2, 3 and 5. Its copied
  exits left before the 5 November fall that its day-long copies rode
  (FIND-REVIEW-6, F2–F3).
- **The disposition trap.** Copied exits raised the average win against the
  average loss (0.96 → 1.12 for every candidate) while the money got slightly
  worse. Only the net per copy measures money.
- **Liquidations.** A follower at a lower leverage would not be liquidated with
  the trader. The whole-copy line (every liquidation fill closes the copy)
  changes every group by under 3 bps a copy, so the reading of a liquidation
  does not matter here.
- **These weeks were seen before.** FIND-6 looked at them once. Any pattern
  here (for example rule 7's top minus rest of the change, +40 ± 24, p 0.10,
  exploring) is a candidate at most, never a finding.

## 5. What stays unknown

- Whether a ranking rule picks traders whose exits a follower can use. On
  these weeks the change from copying exits differs between top and rest by
  more than its error for no rule (p 0.085–0.99, exploring), and the largest
  changes rest on one account.
- How the 20 copies held at the cut ended.
- What second-level prices would show for the 44% of copies held under
  30 minutes.
- A maker follower, or a follower who sizes or leverages differently. Only the
  taker setting is priced.

## 6. Questions, each with a recommendation

1. **Should the idea "copy the exits of disciplined traders" get a study of its
   own?** *Recommendation:* only as a later batch on new weeks, with its rules
   written first, the exits copied as here, and the copies held under about
   5 minutes priced by the second (R8's one-second marks are the way). Not on
   these weeks: decisions 2026-11-12 rule out re-running FIND-6 or trying other
   numbers on them.
2. **Should the forward paper-follow log record each alert's result both ways
   (held 24 h and exits copied)?** *Recommendation:* yes, if the log does not
   already. This check shows the two readings can differ by 30–40 bps a copy
   for a group. The decision is the owner's, at the plan page.
3. **The supervisor's sample re-pricing.** The copies table holds, per copy,
   the exits, the legs (h, part, kind) and every result. *Recommendation:* draw
   the sample from it, including some of the 20 copies marked at the cut and
   some of the 79 with a partial liquidation.
