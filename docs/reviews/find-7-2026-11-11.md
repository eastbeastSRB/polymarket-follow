## For the owner, in one page (completed after the look)

*What is tested.* Some accounts buy (or sell) one market in many pieces, one
after another — an algorithm working a large order. If the price keeps moving
their way for a while, a follower who joins after the fifth piece might still
gain more than a taker pays for the round trip. The study tests exactly that:
from the fifth order plus about 80 seconds (the time a follower needs to see it
and act), hold 30 minutes, pay a taker's fees, spread and funding — is the
average result above zero, counting each day once and each account once?

*What has been done.* The rules were written, tested and checked before any
result was looked at. On the real data only counts exist so far: 4,285 such
runs by 357 accounts on 68 days (2026-08-31 to 11-06), 1,103 of them in the
trading competition's first days; 81 accounts made at least 10 outside the
competition — the list the new weeks would test.

*Next.* A fresh reviewer reads the rules; then the one look.

*The result.* Not yet.

## Potential problems, and how each is handled

As `rules.md` §10: the follower's own order and faster machines (the
doubled-cost run; the recorded book at CONFIRM); the mark follows outside
prices, so a run's push on Polymarket's own book shows in it poorly (stated;
the book at CONFIRM); an order's taker share counts fills after the trigger
(stated; CONFIRM uses the same rule); the busiest accounts mostly set apart
(stated); runs farmed for the competition (P(a) and P(b) required); a few
accounts carry the runs (the account test, one vote per account,
leave-one-out); runs of one day move together (the day test); the price table
from two November mornings (the doubled-cost run).
