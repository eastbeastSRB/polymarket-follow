## For the owner, in one page (completed after the look)

*What is tested.* Some accounts buy (or sell) one market in many pieces, one
after another — an algorithm working a large order. If the price keeps moving
their way for a while, a follower who joins after the fifth piece might still
gain more than a taker pays for the round trip. The study tests exactly that:
from the fifth order plus about 80 seconds (the time a follower needs to see it
and act), hold 30 minutes, pay a taker's fees, spread and funding — is the
average result above zero, counting each day once and each account once?

*What has been done.* The rules were written, tested, read by a fresh reviewer
and corrected before any result was looked at. On the real data only counts
exist: 4,005 such runs by 290 accounts of the all-time volume board on 68 days
(2026-08-31 to 11-06), 1,062 of them in the trading competition's first days;
77 of those accounts made at least 10 outside the competition — the list the
new weeks would test.

*Two guards the review added.* Only accounts chosen for their volume count
(accounts that entered our pool through a profit board were partly chosen for
the very moves this study scores). And because the exchange's reference price
catches up with an order book only over minutes, a follower could look better
on paper than at the book: so the idea is kept for the exams only if it also
survives doubled costs, and a second version entered after the run has gone
quiet is printed beside.

## Potential problems, and how each is handled

As `rules.md` §10, the first corrected after the review: **the mark lags a
run's own push** — it is the median of C1 (the index plus a 150-second average
of the book's premium), C2 (the local book) and C3 (outside feeds), so when a
run pushes the book the mark follows C1, minutes late; a follower pays the
book, and the mark-based result is too kind by the part not yet caught up
(not measurable before the cut) → the doubled-cost condition, the print entered
once the run is silent, the recorded book at CONFIRM. Also: the follower's own
order and faster machines; an order's taker share counts fills after the
trigger; the busiest accounts mostly set apart; runs farmed for the
competition; a few accounts carry the runs; runs of one day move together; the
price table from two November mornings; a weak random-trade placebo.
