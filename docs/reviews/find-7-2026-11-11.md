## For the owner, in one page

*Words.* A **bp** is 0.01%. A **taker** trades at once and pays the fee and
the gap between buying and selling prices; a round trip here costs about 11
bps. A **run** is an account sending at least five orders on one side of one
market within half an hour — an algorithm working a large order.

**The question.** When an algorithm buys (or sells) one market in many
pieces, does the price keep moving its way long enough that a follower who
joins after the fifth piece — about 80 seconds late, the time to see it and
act — still earns more than the round trip costs?

**The answer: no.** On 4,005 such runs by 290 accounts of the all-time volume
board, over 68 days (2026-08-31 to 11-06):

1. **A follower lost about 10 bps (0.10%) a trade** after a taker's costs:
   −9.7 bps on the average day, −10.8 bps on the average account; 218 of 290
   accounts lost, 72 gained. Chance cannot explain a loss this steady (p
   below 0.0001). The same without the trading competition's days, without
   its boosted markets, and in the whole pool.
2. **Why: the price did not keep moving the algorithm's way.** After the fifth
   order it moved about +1 bp more than after a random trade in the same hour
   — inside the noise — while a round trip costs about 11. Nor did it snap
   back afterwards (the hour after the exit: +1.4 / −1.8 bps, noise).
   [Final review, FIND-REVIEW-B, F6: "+1 bp more" is the day mean (+1.31);
   the account mean is −1.74 — between −2 and +1 bp, inside the noise.]
3. **No variant helps:** leaving after 60 minutes (−8 / −14 bps), leaving when
   the algorithm goes quiet (−10 / −9), entering only once it is quiet (−12 /
   −14). Even at a maker's lower cost the result is about zero (−1 / −3 bps,
   not different from zero). At doubled costs, −21 bps.
4. **How sure:** the test could have seen a gain of about 8 bps a trade; the
   best the data allow is still a loss of about 5–7 bps a trade.

**What it means:** entry 7 is **not registered** — it does not go to the
exams, and it adds nothing to the number of ideas tested there. An honest
"no": with the delay a follower has, the trail an algorithm leaves on this
exchange is already in the price.

This is a statement about the past, not advice on what to trade.

- **The mark lags a run's own push** (C1 = the index plus a 150-second EMA of
  the book's premium; C2 the local book; C3 outside feeds): the mark-based
  result would be too kind to a follower who pays the book. The result is
  negative anyway; `quiet30`, entered after the push, is lower still.
- **Accounts chosen on profit:** the notional board decides; the whole pool
  gives the same.
- **The competition's farmed volume:** P(a) and P(b) — the same.
- **A few accounts carry the runs** (the busiest 20 hold half of P): the
  account test and leave-one-out — the same.
- **The price table is from November** and the follower's own order is not in
  past data: these can only make the true cost higher, and the result is a
  loss already.
- **The busiest accounts are mostly set apart** (truncated captures): stated
  in §7.
- **The dry runs on real prices** (placebo data, three, logged): not looks
