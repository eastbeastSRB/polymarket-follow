## For the owner, in one page (completed after the look)

*What is tested.* Some directional traders usually keep a position for six
hours or more. If a follower had copied each new position of theirs about 80
seconds late and held it for a day, would that have beaten a random trade —
the average of every public trade in the same market and hour, taken long and
short in the trader's own usual mix? That yardstick gives no credit for simply
being long in a rising market. The result is counted in "risk units" — the
day's result divided by the market's usual daily move — so that a quiet market
and a wild one weigh alike; it is shown in bps (1 bp = 0.01%) beside.

*Who.* Only traders the style label called directional, whose median hold
over the 30 days before the week of each trade was six hours or more; only
accounts on the all-time notional (volume) board, because the others entered
our list by being profitable, and that profit is partly the very move scored
here. From 2026-09-03 to 2026-11-07: 981 trades of 73 traders on 64 days; 951
can be scored.

*What it can see.* On random trades the test passes far less often than its
nominal rate (0.2% instead of 2.5%): it is careful. The price: on these 65
days it can only see an edge of roughly 0.2 risk units — about 47 bps a trade
over a day. A smaller edge would most likely be missed.

*What comes next.* A fresh reviewer reads the rules; then, at the
supervisor's go, the frozen code runs once.
