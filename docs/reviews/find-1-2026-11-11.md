## For the owner, in one page

**The question.** Do some traders keep doing better than others? Not by their
own profit, but by what a follower would have got: copying each new position
about 80 seconds late, holding it a day, and comparing it with a random trade
in the same market and hour (so that simply being long in a rising market
earns nothing). If the traders who did well in one set of weeks also did well
in another, "which trader to follow" would have an answer. This is the gate of
round 2: the race between ranking rules (entry 6) runs only if it passes.

**How.** The 99 directional traders of the all-time volume board with at
least 10 such trades in odd calendar weeks and 10 in even weeks — 7,252 trades
on 152 days, 2026-06-01 to the cut (2026-11-07). Each trader got one score from
the odd weeks and one from the even weeks; a day of trading counts once, and
each trade is measured in "risk units" — its result divided by the market's
usual daily move — so that quiet and wild markets weigh alike.

**The answer: no. FAILS.** The two orders barely agree: a rank correlation of
**0.07** (0 = no relation, 1 = the same order). Shuffling the traders at random
gives one at least as far from zero about half the time (**p = 0.51**); the
rule needed under 5%. The top fifth picked on one set of weeks did a little
better than the bottom fifth on the other (by 0.12 and 0.11 of a usual day's
move), but no more than chance gives (by the tests printed beside, p 0.49 and
0.90). Without the trading competition's days the small agreement disappears
(−0.02); without its boosted markets, 0.06.

**Every other view says the same** (deciding nothing): in bps 0.14 (p 0.16);
each trade weighed alike 0.11 (p 0.30); at each trader's usual horizon 0.04; at
three days 0.03; with every account we hold 0.02; keeping only trades whose day
ends inside their own week 0.07. The closest: only the weeks since the launch
week (2026-08-31), 56 traders, 0.23 (p 0.10) — still short of the line, on
about half the traders.

**What it means.** As you ruled (decisions 2026-11-10 (2), on IDEAS-REVIEW's
question 4): entry 6 does not run, and ranking traders stops for now, to be
reopened when the universe holds six more months. The app shows each trader's measures with their evidence and
ranks no one. Nothing from this study goes to the exams.

**What it does not mean.** It does not prove that all traders are alike. These
weeks could see only a large difference: one where traders' true results
spread by about **0.26 of a usual day's move (about 46 bps a trade over a
day)**, caught four times in five. The best estimate of the real spread is
0.09 (36 bps), likely between 0 and 0.20 (0–52 bps). A smaller difference may
exist; these weeks cannot show it — and the exams' six weeks would most likely
not have confirmed one that small (they could see a gap between the best and
the worst fifth only above about 72–142 bps).

**One side observation (decides nothing):** 9 of the 20 traders on top in the
odd weeks were also on top in the even weeks, where chance puts about 4; at
the bottom, 3 (chance about 4). It was noticed after the look, in one corner of
a 25-cell table, and the gap between the fifths stayed small — at most an idea
to test on new weeks.
