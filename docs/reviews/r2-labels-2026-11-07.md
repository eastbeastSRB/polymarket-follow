# R2-LABELS — blind style labels for the 100 holdout accounts

Research report, 2026-11-07 (UTC), by R2-LABELS (Sonnet 5.5, xhigh). Raw fills
only: no API call, no web. Point-in-time evidence; it decides nothing
(docs/reviews/README.md). I never opened anything of R2-STYLE's. The labels
are in the supervisor folder: `workers\R2-LABELS\labels.csv` (100 holdout

## How I read the accounts

Window 2026-10-24T00:00Z to 2026-11-07T00:00Z for all. Scripts (in
`workers\R2-LABELS\`, none decides a label): `lib.cjs` loads the pages
(newest first, `w` pages ignored, duplicates dropped) and builds order events,
orders and position cycles; `census.cjs` prints one count line per account;
`view.cjs` prints the compact view I read; `dormant.cjs` lists accounts with no
fill in the window; `events-by-size.cjs`, `orders-by-day.cjs` and
`instrument-flow.cjs` are small drill-downs (events of one size, orders per day,
bought/sold/net per instrument; named `q1/q2/q3` when I used them [Final review:
renamed]); `forced-only.cjs` counts accounts whose window fills are all forced
[Final review: added]; `addlabel.cjs` only checks the label word and appends my
row; `verify.cjs` checks the finished file.

The view shows, per account: captured span, fills, maker share, fees and gross
pnl, forced fills; instruments with buys/sells and maker/taker; the timeline
(order events, or action groups when long); holds of closed cycles (buckets,
median); events by hour and by day, the longest gaps; instruments held at
once; sizes and notionals repeated; maker side switches and flips. I read it
against §4–§5 and wrote each row at once.

## Hard cases and why

- **`0284a952` — mixed, low.** Reads like a taker scalper (2 instruments,
  one-sided, minutes to hours, quiet days), but trade size grows about 1% each
  cycle (96.7, 97.7, 98.0, 98.8, 99.9 …), 34 of 35 cycles won, and round trips
  come every 3–5 minutes in bursts. A person or a bot; I put it near even odds.
- **`9493c286` — mixed.** All 10 cycles held 10.4–14.4 hours, each entered and
  left in 4–8 uneven slices over 10–30 minutes, about 2 decisions a day, no
  size ever repeated. A slow systematic directional trader (§4 allows it) or a
  bot.
- **`5496914c` — mixed.** One 90-minute burst: 13 round trips of about $50k
  held 18 s to 15 min, 69% maker through only 33 orders, gross −$46 on $1.15M,
  fees $307. A limit-order scalper or volume churn; its whole history is 10 h.
- **`cd733fea` — too few.** Three taker round trips of 2–29 minutes in equal
  slices, gross pnl ≈ 0, fees $54: scalper or churn, three trips cannot say.
- **`d0b9b1af` — directional scalp, medium.** An hour of 7 round trips of
  24 s to 5 min over 5 instruments (ids ascending), then slow trades of hours
  to days. Pnl mostly positive, sizes vary, so I called it a person sweeping
  markets, not a churner.
- **`3993f7a9`, `adcd084f`, `d294eaf1` — directional, medium.** Size rises
  after wins and falls after losses, or round-dollar clips ($4000, $4500);
  both are normal for a person sizing by equity or by dollars.
- **`7c97eb77` — directional scalp, medium.** 4,148 events from only 50
  orders: large resting limit orders each filled hundreds of times (the §4
  "one large resting order" case). Median hold 50 min, six holds of days.
- **`c455dbd3` — algorithm, medium.** 87 cycles, almost each closed and
  reopened on the other side seconds later in a fixed dollar clip
  ($8000/$12000/$15000 repeated 20, 12, 10 times) — but it rests every night
  (quiet 21–08 UTC), which a bot can do too. [Final review: was "87
  stop-and-reverse flips"; see F6.]
- **`b8c6cd09` — algorithm, not churner.** 30 round trips, 18 closed after
  exactly 5.0 s, always $1000 notional, gross ≈ 0, below fees. That is churn
  made machine-regular, which §4 says is "algorithm".
- **Maker-heavy books, market maker or algorithm?** `f0c33734`, `fb71f920`,
  `bd74e18c`, `f544cab9` (100% maker, rebates, 9–17 instruments held at once,
  adjusted in tiny steps, mostly inherited inventories that never reach zero)
  I called algorithm ("a book run in parallel"); `762d2937`, `84699add`
  (95–96% maker, 60 instruments, $1000 clips, 1100+ cycles flat to flat) and
  `1a12ff3f`, `1a2286f1`, `1a32426b` (81–89% maker, 200+ flips through zero)
  market maker; `eefaac3b` (56% maker) market maker, `6cd074ec` (47%)
  algorithm. My line was "maker fills are the majority". All are
  non-directional either way.

## Where the definitions were unclear or did not fit

- §4 "Unclassified — too few": "Too few decisions in the window … (one
  position cycle; two quick round trips)". No count. I used: at most about 5
  orders or 2 cycles opened and closed in the window, and the story not
  clearly one label → too few; a directional label needed 3 or more full
  cycles with person-like signs. Eight of the 20 are medium confidence, and
  several (`e4cd513d`, `f1ed6b2a`, `a553d641`) look directional.
- §3.2 "Forced fills are not decisions" against §5.1 "No fill in the window →
  no label". Three accounts have fills but only forced ones (`a28cc106`,
  `b3199f7c`, `f92fe7e6`); two have a single close of an inherited position
  (`1c9b12d2`, `afcd816a`). I gave all five too few. A forced-only window may
  deserve its own dormant-like flag.
- §4 Directional horizon: "minutes — scalp; hours — intraday; days — swing;
  weeks — position" has no numbers and many accounts mix holds. I took the
  median hold of closed cycles: under about 1 h scalp, to about 1 day
  intraday, to about 1 week swing. Borderline: `9566411a` (median 56 min,
  written intraday), `a3a1f52e` (15 h, edge of swing), `7c97eb77`.
- §4 Market maker "small inventory": real maker-only books carry large
  inventories that move both ways. The text does not say which side such a
  book falls on (see above).
- §1 "one label per account per window" for accounts whose whole history is a
  few hours (`0ff801df`, `5496914c`, `adcd084f`, `8f893ae0`): the label is of
  one session, not of a style.
- Many fills on a short window are partial fills of one resting order
  (`5496914c`, `7c97eb77`, `8f893ae0`); the event/order split handled it, but
  an order-event count overstates activity for maker-led accounts.

## What stays unknown

- For the ten partly captured accounts, the older days: a daily rhythm or
  two-sidedness could differ. For the 40 "none" I know only "no fill in the
  window".
- Whether the directional accounts are really people. Sparse, varied,
  one-sided taker trading is the sign I used; I cannot see sleep gaps on
  accounts with a few trades.
- The `w` pages (older window samples) were ignored as told.
