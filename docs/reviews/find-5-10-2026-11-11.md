## For the owner, in one page

*Words.* A **bp** is 0.01%. **Random trades** are the yardstick: three
public trades picked by chance in the same market and hour as each entry
(R8 drew them). A **risk unit** is the market's usual 24-hour move; 0.1 risk
units is a tenth of it. **FIND** is the search on data up to 2026-11-07;
what passes goes to the two exams on new weeks. **m** is the number of
ideas sent to the exams; each one makes the pass line stricter for all.

**Entry 10, R8's "US open" cell: it does not hold.** R8 found that the top
50 traders' entries opened between 12:00 and 16:00 UTC did 38 bps worse
than random trades. Measured again on 344 of those 387 entries with the
rule book's yardstick, over 24 hours, they did about the same as random
trades: +0.05 and +0.02 risk units (one mean per day, one per trader), on
the wrong side of zero for the idea, p 0.65 and 0.83. Where R8's figure
came from shows step by step. On the same entries and the same random
trades, R8's −38 bps comes out again to the digit; read the rule book's
way, one mean per day and one per trader, it is −28 and −19 bps. Give
those random trades the trader's usual direction by a coin, instead of the
side they really took, and it shrinks to −4 and +0.5 bps. So R8's finding
was mostly about the random trades at that hour, whose own side did better
than a coin over the next hours, and not about the traders. Even by R8's
own figures, the test with one mean per trader would not have passed
(p 0.29).

**Entry 5, the first minute: no sign of news, and the yardstick explains
the rest.** In the 60 seconds after one of the 50 enters, the price does
not move their way at all on average (about 0 bps). After a random trade it
moves about 1.5–2 bps in that trade's own direction within 10 seconds. So
against random trades the entries come out lower: −1.45 bps (day means) and
−3.00 bps (trader means), p 0.03. That is the opposite of the idea, which
needed them higher. The same test run on random trades in place of the
entries gives a negative number of the same size, so it describes the
yardstick (a price follows the side of a random trade for a few seconds),
not these traders. What the result does say: no sign that the 50's entries
carry news that shows in the price in the first minute. No follower could
act inside that minute anyway.

**What this means.** Neither idea goes to the exams, so m gains nothing
from this study, and the lines stay easier for the ideas that remain. It
does not say that the 50 trade well or badly; only that these two patterns
are not there by the rule book's measure. Nothing here is trading advice.
