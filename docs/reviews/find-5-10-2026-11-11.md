## For the owner, in one page

*Words.* A **bp** is 0.01%. **"Random trades"** are the yardstick: what a
trade picked by chance in the same market and hour did. **FIND** is the data
up to 2026-11-07, where ideas may be looked for; what passes goes to the two
exams on new weeks.

**Two questions, both on R8's six weeks of the top 50 traders' entries.**

1. **Entry 5 — the first minute.** In the 60 seconds after one of these
   traders opens a position, does the price move their way more than it does
   after a random trade? If yes, their entries carry news (or their own
   push) — gone, most likely, before a person could act, but a sign that
   something is there. Measured on R8's one-second prices, 1,241 entries.
2. **Entry 10 — the US-open cell, measured again.** R8 found that entries
   opened between 12:00 and 16:00 UTC did worse than random trades
   (−38 bps), but against a yardstick the owner has since replaced. The cell
   is measured again with round 2's only yardstick, over a day (24 hours),
   on 344 of R8's 387 entries (the rest sit in markets or cells the rule book
   leaves out). It may well not hold.

**How it is kept honest.** Every rule — the populations, the yardstick, the
statistic, the line to pass, what is printed beside — was written down and
fingerprinted before any result was computed (`rules.md`, fingerprint below);
a fresh reviewer reads them next; then one run, once, and nothing changed
after it. Each line passes only if both of the rule book's tests (one per day,
one per trader) say so at the 5% level in the expected direction, and the
result also holds without the trading competition's days and without its
boosted markets.

**Where things stand:** rules ready, six questions for the supervisor (each
