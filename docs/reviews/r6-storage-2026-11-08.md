**How the BSON column was measured.** My own size calculator (BSON spec: type byte
+ name + NUL + value; strings 4 + length + 1; array elements carry their index as a
key; Date 8, ObjectId 12, int32 4, double 8, Decimal128 16) agrees byte for byte
with MongoDB's `bson` library on nine shapes (`out/test-lib-bson.txt`). BSON is
**bigger** than the JSON: a fill by 13%, a book by 77% (every `[price, qty]` pair
becomes a document with keys "0" and "1" inside an array whose elements carry
keys). The shapes are **candidates** — Zod parses the reply, the stored document
is that parsed object plus `account` and `seenAt` — not a schema decision.

**What each choice buys on a fill** (`out/an-sizes.txt`; BSON mean, then the
modelled snappy size per document):

| Shape | BSON | on disk, data only (grouped / arrival order) |
|---|---|---|
| F1 as received (recommended) | 471 B | 124 / 147 B (model) |
| F2 lean: no `fee_asset`, `hash`, `builder_fee`, `total_fee` (constant or derivable) | 387 B | 117 / 138 B |
| F2b lean + the address as 20 binary bytes | 365 B | 114 / 120 B |
| F3 F2b + one- or two-letter field names | 258 B | 105 / 109 B |

After compression the whole ladder saves 15–25%, and the model is ±25%, so
**F1 is enough**: it is the Zod schema as it is, with no mapping code. Money stays
a decimal string (code-rules): the eight money strings of the sample fill take
107 B as strings and would take 128 B as Decimal128, and a string round-trips
exactly.

**From BSON to disk: what MongoDB's documentation says and what I measured.**
MongoDB's manual (WiredTiger page, read 2026-11-08): "By default, WiredTiger uses
block compression with the snappy compression library for most collections and
prefix compression for all indexes"; zlib and zstd are selectable, also per
collection ("You can override this on a per-collection basis when creating
collections", mongod reference); the journal is snappy-compressed; checkpoints
every 60 seconds; the cache is "the larger of either: 50% of (RAM - 1GB), or
0.256 GB" (here 31.9 GB of RAM: up to ~15 GB; the lab's mongod uses ~1 GB). The
manual gives no ratio, so:

- I wrote a snappy compressor (round-trip tested, `scripts/lib-bson.cjs`) and cut
  the real documents into 32 KB blocks like a WiredTiger leaf page. The model
  gives **3.2× (arrival order across accounts) to 4.7× (one busy account's
  fills in a row)** on fills, 1.9× on named leaderboard rows, 2.3× on books, 2.1×
  on portfolios (`out/an-sizes.txt`, `an-contig.txt`, `an-lb.txt`, `an-books.txt`,
  `an-light.txt`). zstd compresses 1.5–1.7× better still.
- **Calibration against the lab's real server.** On rows shaped like the lab's
  `price_snapshots` (invented values; its data was not read) the same model gives
  2.60×, where the lab's real ratio is 1.79× (8,301,251 rows, 1,544 MB → 861 MB,
  its `how-it-works.md` §8). So **real ≈ model × 0.69** (WiredTiger page headers,
  per-record cells, block rounding): I applied it to every snappy figure above and
  in §Q2 (`out/cal-lab-shape.txt`). A fill therefore compresses ~2.4×, not 3.2–4.7×.
- **Index entries** from the lab's real index files (data-path listing, file sizes
  only; the three big ones, identified as `price_snapshots`' by that sum matching
  the lab's stated 591 MB, not by name): 42.1 B per row for
  `{marketId (28 chars), ts}`, 19.1 B for `_id`, 10.2 B for `ts`. For a fill I use
  **19 B (`_id`) + 45 B (the unique key `{account, timestamp, instrument_id,
  trade_id}`)**, which is more than my own prefix-compression model (21.5 B,
  because inserts land all over a B-tree, not at its end).
- **Fixed overheads of a server, not ours:** two journal files of 100 MB (201 MB),
  `diagnostic.data` 502 MB (a capped ring of 10 MB files), `mongod.log` 434 MB
  growing ~4.8 MB a day since 2026-08-09 (no rotation configured) — on C:.

### Q3. What to keep, and for how long

**Answer.** Keep **parsed records**, not raw replies; keep for ever what cannot be
re-read; keep what can be re-read only as far as it is cheap; keep the call log
as files, not documents.

*What the API can give back later* (R1, external-apis): all fills of an account
back to its first one (10 tokens a page of 100, a cursor valid for hours);
`/v1/info/pnl` and `/equity` series to launch; funding, klines, mark history and
the tape to launch (BTC: 2026-05-29). *What it cannot:* **leaderboard standings
over time** (each board is a "now" snapshot, only the top 1,000 can be listed);
**books, bbo, tickers, fees, instruments at an instant** ("now only");
**competition standings at an instant**; and **everything we observe ourselves**
(when our poll first saw a fill, the alert's delay, what was shown). A position's
history is rebuilt from fills (`previous_size` chains); its unrealized PnL at a
past instant from the mark history.

| Collection (names are illustrative) | Keep | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `leaderboard_snapshots` — one document per board per read, 1,000 entries, the page timestamps kept | **for ever** | cannot be re-read |
| `paper_*` — entries, instants (books), nulls, votes; append-only, never edited | **for ever** | Iron Rule 4; books cannot be re-read |
| `alerts` — the feed | **for ever** (small: 15 MB a year at 20 typical favourites, 77 MB at 100) | the delay and "unseen" are ours |
| `style_labels` — label, window, coverage, reason, classifier version and hash | **for ever** | tiny; R2 asks for the version beside every label |

| `fills` of an un-favourited trader | keep (the paper log refers to them) | |
| `favourites`, settings, poller state, heartbeat | for ever (latest state, a few KB) | |
| `api_usage_hourly` — calls, tokens, 429s, latency per endpoint per hour | for ever (20 MB a year) | R1's call log is what made the bucket fit possible |

### Q5. Backup

**Answer.** A daily full dump of our database only, written by a small Node script
with the project's own driver (the lab's pattern), to F: and a second disk,
verified by a manifest every day and by a restore into a scratch database every
week.

**Why not `mongodump`:** it is not installed (`where` finds neither it nor
`mongosh`/`mongorestore`; `C:\Program Files\MongoDB` holds only `Server`); it is a
separate download ("MongoDB Database Tools", versioned 100.x). The manual says
that without `--oplog` a dump taken while writes happen "will not reflect a single
moment in time", `--oplog` "only works against nodes that maintain an oplog" (a
replica set; this server has none) and fails with `--db`. So on this server
`mongodump --db polymarket_follow` is a per-collection scan with the same gaps as
a script. The lab uses a script too (its `tools/backup.cjs`; its reasons are in
its playbook R10, which I did not read). If the owner prefers the tool, install it and use `--db
polymarket_follow --gzip` into a dated folder: the folder layout, schedule and
checks below stay the same.

**The script (designed from the lab's, copied not shared):**

- Reads every collection **except a `SKIPPED` list that names its reason** (so a new
  collection is backed up by default, the lab's choice), 1,000 documents at a time,
  into `<root>\<UTC day>\<collection>.jsonl.gz` (Extended JSON, dates stay dates)
  plus `indexes.json` (`listIndexes`), and writes **`manifest.json` last** (file
  names, sizes, SHA-256, the cut-off instant, document counts). A folder without a
  manifest is incomplete.
- **A consistent cut for the append-only collections:** the dump takes only
  documents with an `_id` older than the start of the run minus 60 s
  (`ObjectId.createFromTime`). Mutable collections (favourites, alerts' shown
  flags, state) are small and are read whole. Cross-collection gaps are healed by
  the app: fills are re-readable, and every reference is checked at restore.

## 2. Variations weighed

- **Raw replies vs parsed records:** parsed (Q3). Raw doubles the bytes with
  repeats and is what Iron Rule 3 says to drop on failure; `.passthrough()` keeps
  new fields.
- **Field names:** the API's own (the Zod schema is the database schema) vs short
  names: short names save 8% on disk after compression (Q1); not worth a mapping
  layer.
- **Address as string vs 20-byte binary:** saves 22 B of BSON, 3–19 B after
  compression; kept as a lower-case string, readable in any tool.
- **Money as string vs Decimal128:** 107 vs 128 B per fill; strings round-trip
  exactly and are the code rule.
- **One document per fill vs one bucket per account-day:** a bucket saves the
  per-document `_id` and its two index entries (~80 B of 260) but makes the
  dedupe by key an array operation; not at these volumes.
- **A leaderboard snapshot as one document of 1,000 entries vs one document per
  row:** one document (173–261 KB, far below 16 MiB): no per-row `_id` or index
  (~64 B of 196), one write, one read, and the page timestamps stay beside it.
  Named entries (the Zod shape) vs positional arrays: 573 vs 366 MB a year at plan
  B; named kept for simplicity.
- **Book: full depth-100 vs cut at $50,000 or $10,000 a side:** full is 2.1 KB, cut
  0.4 KB; an entry is 20.6 vs 9.4 KB. Full needs no code and answers questions
  asked later (R3 priced $50,000 as a sensitivity and found some index books
  that 100 levels cannot fill); cut at the first use of its numbers.
- **Snapshot cadence:** daily vs hourly vs 15-minute (Q2); daily.
- **The call log:** every call as a document (4+ GB a year) vs rolling files plus
  hourly aggregates; files.
- **Backup tool:** Node script (chosen) vs `mongodump` (not installed; same
  consistency) vs copying the data directory (needs the shared server stopped, so
  it stops the lab) vs a volume snapshot (not available on this setup) vs a
  replica set for `--oplog` (a second mongod and an oplog for nothing).
- **Backup copies:** E: + F: (decided 2026-11-06) vs F: + D: (§7 question 3).
- **Full dump vs once-written day files:** full first (simple), day files when a
  dump passes ~1 GB.
- **Location:** A / B / C above.
- **Compression:** snappy (default, no change) vs zstd for the large collections
  (1.5× smaller; per-collection, so possible on the shared server without a config
  change; to be measured first).
- **Not MongoDB at all** (SQLite through `node:sqlite`, one file on F:): out of
  scope, the owner chose MongoDB and the lab's stack.

## 3. Possibilities found

1. **zstd per collection on the shared server** (`createCollection` with
   `storageEngine: { wiredTiger: { configString: "block_compressor=zstd" } }`): the
   mongod reference allows a per-collection override; the model says 1.5× smaller
   on fills and leaderboard rows. Worth taking for `fills`, `leaderboard_snapshots`
   and `paper_*` once the throwaway measurement confirms it.
2. **Clustered collections** (new in 5.3): store the documents in `_id` order and
   drop the separate `_id` index (19 B a fill), but the key must be `_id`, and a
   collection cannot be converted later. Not now.
3. **Time-series collections** compress with zstd by default, but "MongoDB doesn't
   support … Unique indexes" on them, updates are limited to the meta field, and a
   document is capped at 4 MB: wrong for fills (dedupe by key). Fit only for
   something we do not store.
4. **OpenTimestamps on the daily manifest**, as the lab does with Node built-ins
   only: a public timestamp that the paper-follow log existed in that state that
   day, a verifiable form of Iron Rule 4. Cheap to copy.
5. **A capped collection for rejected replies** (64 MB): bounded, self-pruning,
   gives the next schema change its examples.
6. **Hourly API-usage aggregates** (20 MB a year) instead of a call log: R1's
   bucket fit needed the call log only to be reproduced; the aggregate shows the
   429s and the token use the budget rule needs.
7. **A digest row for machine-tempo favourites:** R4 says algorithms "stay in the
   feed"; the median machine-tempo account makes 296 decisions a day (0.12 MB a
   day of alert rows), the busiest captured 3,668 (1.5 MB a day, as many bytes as
   its fills). One row an hour (counts, net change) per such favourite keeps the
   feed readable and the database small.
8. **`fs.statfsSync`** reads free space on every drive in Node 24 here (tested): the
   disk alarms need no extra tool.
9. **The two weakest links in the hardware, found by looking:** E: is the NVMe's
   second partition (no independence from C:), and the one HDD (F:) holds both the
   only copy of the research capture and the lab's mirror.
