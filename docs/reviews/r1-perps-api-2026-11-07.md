**Headline.** The public API gives everything the follow project needs

Four things differ from what the founding docs assumed: (1) the rate limit is a

will not "pass" JavaScript's limit; (3) `previous_size` on a fill is the
position **before the whole order**, not before that fill; (4) a fill's
`trade_id` **is** the tape's trade id, so a study can exclude chosen accounts'
trades from the tape. The alert delay is dominated by the poll interval; the
API's own lag is about 1–1.5 s.

  **`hash` "0x" on all 4,063** (when it is not "0x" is unknown; the docs say
  "0x" if not yet mined).
- **`previous_size` / `previous_entry_price` are the state before the whole order
  event**, signed (long positive). All fills of one order in one matching event

### Q6. The IDs

**They do not grow and they do not pass 2^53.** 4,063 `trade_id` and
`order_id` values (and the 300 saved fills): spread evenly over 0…
9,007,199,254,740,991 (deciles 408/418/443/362/331/378/412/417/421/473 for
trade_id), max 99.98% of the limit, **none above it**, 88.7% have 16 digits,
correlation with the timestamp −0.02, consecutive-in-time ids go up and down
(141 up, 158 down) — random 53-bit values drawn per trade/order ("probabilistically
unique", the spec's own wording; spec `x-js-safe-integer`: all v1 ids fit a JS
number). The saved fill's 8,522,514,252,061,563 was one random draw, not a
position on a counter. Consecutive ids exist only inside one event (an order
sweeping two makers gets id and id+1). **Consequences:** no overflow date; ids
are exact in a JS number, so a Zod `z.number().int().safe()` is enough (a value
above it fails loudly, which is what we want); never sort or cursor by id; the
collision odds for random 53-bit ids (1 − exp(−n²/2^54), n ids drawn
uniformly from 2^53 values) are 5.6e-5 at 10^6 trades and 0.43 at 10^8
(`out/an-ids-2.txt`). WebSocket: the trades channel carries `tid` as a JSON
integer (16 digits in the raw frame); `oid` appears only on the private fills
channel (not touched). `out/an-ids-1.txt`, `an-ids-2.txt`.

### Q7. The portfolio

Fields: `positions[]` {instrument_id, symbol, size (signed string), entry_price,
unrealized_pnl, return_on_equity}, `equity`, `coverage`{metadata_available,
missing_instrument_ids, collateral}, `timestamp`. **No leverage, margin mode,
available margin or liquidation price** — the share-card route says leverage is
"only returned to the authenticated owner". Live (timestamp = request time).
**Replacing fills polling (10) by portfolio polling (5) plus a fills read on a
change:** half the cost per poll and fresher (it leads fills), but it can miss
(replay of real fills, `out/sim-portfolio-*.txt`, windows with ≥ 1 event):
**blind** (size unchanged at both polls: an open and a close, or a reduce and an
add that net out) — 0.0–0.6% for market-maker-like accounts at any interval, but
**6.8% at 10 s and 5.6% at 120 s for a scalper** (0xa404FA…, 1.5 events/h in
bursts) and **12% at 60 s for a 52 events/h account**; slow accounts 0–1.9%.
**Compressed** (several events collapse into one visible size change; the fills
read afterwards recovers them): 33–95% of events for busy accounts, 0–88% for
slow ones (bursts of orders within seconds). Cheap ways to catch a blind window: (1) read the fills on **any
equity-independent trigger**: none of the cheap fields works — `equity` changed in
60 of 60 ticks (marks), and the leaderboard address row is stale and flaps
(Q3); (2) keep a fills read at a slow cadence as a safety net (e.g. each
favourite's fills every 5–10 minutes: 1–2 tokens a minute each) and catch the
rest there; (3) treat the net-zero case as acceptable for directional traders
(blind ≤ 2% at ≤ 60 s) and refuse portfolio-only following for accounts whose
style label is scalper/algo. A fills read triggered right at the change may
return without the new fill (the portfolio leads fills: in 3 of the 12
fill-bearing 5 s ticks the portfolio showed the change a whole tick before the
fills read did; the other 9 were in the same tick): wait ~2 s (also past the 2 s
cache) and read again [Final review F3: "about half the time" was unsupported;
the measured share is 3 of 12 = 25% at 5 s resolution, one account].

  (same interval list): equity samples, the last sample of each bucket
  (23:59:55Z daily). **`/v2/info/pnl`**: intervals **1s 1m 5m 15m 30m 1h 4h 6h
  12h 1d 1w** (`1w` is accepted; the lab's 400 was most likely the missing
  `start_timestamp`, which now answers 400 "missing field"); the reply is the **cumulative total PnL** (equity with
  unrealized, minus net capital), samples at `…:55.000Z` (daily) and the current

- **How far back:** to the account's first known capital event. **Coverage is the
  catch: 9 of 50 leaderboard accounts answered with points, 41 with an empty

  the lifetime history shipped), 40 of the 41 empty ones started earlier — so older
  accounts (most of the top of the leaderboard) have no total-PnL series.
- **Deposits and withdrawals.** Where it works, `equity − total PnL` is the net
  capital, so every deposit/withdrawal shows up as a step to the hour: account
  0xDdeBE6… went 100 → **10,100** (+10,000 on 2026-11-03 ~11:00Z) → **1,749.10**
  (−8,350.90 on 2026-11-04 ~15:00Z), 274 matched hourly samples, 3 distinct net

  *deposits* (denominator = starting equity + **peak** net deposits) but not for
  withdrawals. **For old accounts** the v1 pair gives an approximation:
  equity change − realized pnl = net flow + change in unrealized; on the
  all-time #1 the daily series shows two jumps of $1.58M and $1.36M on
  2026-06-22/23 (deposits) and noise of ±$0.1–0.2M thereafter
  (`out/an-flows.txt`) — large flows are visible, small ones are masked by
  unrealized swings (hourly buckets shrink the error).
- Cost: weight unpublished (fit ≈ 13–15) — assume 15.

### Q11. The WebSocket's public channels

`out/ws1.txt`, `ws2.txt`, `raw/ws1-samples.json`. No authentication needed.
`wss://…/v1/ws`; one message subscribes to many channels; the answer is
`{"id":1,"data":[{"status":"ok"},…]}`. Frames: `{"ch","ts","ets","sq","data"}` —
`ts` server send time, `ets` newest reflected event (0 = unknown), `sq` a single
growing sequence number per connection (not per channel, so gap checks must use
the connection's). **Trades** `data` is an array of {tid, iid, side, p, qty,
settlement, ts, hash} (the spec says object); **bbo** {iid, bp, bq, ap, aq};
**tickers** {iid, idx, mark, last, mid, oi, fr, nxf} pushed 10 times a second per
instrument even when unchanged (~2,974 frames in 5 minutes on one instrument —
prefer bbo). **No account field anywhere** (confirmed on all keys seen). First
frame 250 ms after the subscribe; connect ~1.0 s. Keep-alive: the client ping
`{"id":n,"req":"post","op":{"type":"ping"}}` was answered (pongs); the docs say
connections close after 60 s idle; two 120–150 s connections stayed open, one with
no client ping (busy channels), and a connection with **no subscription and no
message at all stayed open for 68 s** (`out/ws3.txt`) — the 60 s rule was not
enforced in that test. One connect took **61.8 s** to open (the others ~1.0 s;
cause unknown, maybe the per-IP connect limit in `limit-tiers`): allow a long
connect timeout. Reconnect: deliberate close then reopen worked at once, a fresh
subscribe needed. Limits: 50 connections, 100 subscriptions each,
1,000 inbound messages a minute per IP. **Live prices at alert time without
HTTP weight: yes** — keep one connection on `bbo::<iid>` for the favourites'
instruments (a stream of ~4 frames/s for BTC), and read the price when an alert
fires.

### Q14. The feasibility table

**Method.** My share is 500 tokens a minute (half of the published 1,000; the
bucket refills ≈ 16.5/s and bursts ~90–100). **Reserve 100 a minute** (20%):
leaderboard refresh of four windows' top 1,000 = 40 pages × 5 = 200 tokens per
cycle every 15 min (13/min), tickers each minute (2), bbo/book at alerts (~4 per
alert), a margin for retries and the owner's browser. That leaves **400 a minute
for favourites**. Delay = T/2 + API lag 1.0–1.5 s (Q5) + 0.4 s request + ~0.1 s
processing ≈ T/2 + 1.7 s (A, fills) or T/2 + 0.5 s (B′, portfolio alert).
Formulas: A: T = 1.5·N s (10 tokens per favourite per poll). B: 5 tokens per
poll plus ~1 fills read pair per event (3 events/h each): T = 300·N/(400 − N).
C: active fifth polled every T_a, the rest every 4·T_a: tokens = 6,000/T_a (N=50).

| Favourites | A: fills only — T / alert delay | B: portfolio + fills on change — T / delay (alert from the size change; from the fill: +2 s) | C: mix, active ⅕ fast — T_active / T_idle / delay for active |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 7.5 s / **≈ 5.5 s** | 3.8 s / **≈ 2.4 s** | all fast: 3.8 s / 2.4 s |
| 20 | 30 s / **≈ 16.7 s** | 15.8 s / **≈ 8.4 s** | 6.3 s / 25 s / **≈ 3.7 s** |
| 50 | 75 s / **≈ 39 s** | 42.9 s / **≈ 22 s** | 17 s / 69 s / **≈ 9 s** |
| 100 | 150 s / **≈ 77 s** | 100 s / **≈ 50.5 s** | 40 s / 160 s / **≈ 20.5 s** |

Worst case is T + lag (double the T/2 term). B and C miss net-zero windows
(Q7) and need the slow fills safety net. Sources: weights from the spec
(Q1), the bucket from Q2, lag from Q5, miss rates from Q7; the arithmetic is the
formulas above (`out/an-feasible.txt` holds the same numbers).

**Cost of a historical study (addendum).** At 500 tokens a minute (60 fills
pages a minute) and 100 rows a page: **one account's full fill history** = fills /
100 reads × 10 tokens: a directional trader at 1.3 fills/h over the 137 days
since the first fills (~4,300 fills) = 43 reads, 430 tokens, under a minute;
the all-time #1 at 37.7 fills/h (~124,000) = 1,240 reads, 12,400 tokens, ~25
minutes; a market maker at 250 fills/h (~820,000) = 8,200 reads, 82,000 tokens,
~2.7 hours. **The tape around 1,000 entries** = one window request per entry
(`start_timestamp = t`, `end_timestamp = t + delay + margin`, ≤ 100 trades in
nearly every case) = 1,000 × 10 = 10,000 tokens, **20 minutes**; or klines 1m for
the traded instruments: 5 tokens per 1,000 candles, ≤ 233 requests per instrument
for the whole history = 1,165 tokens (2.3 min) per instrument. **Funding
history** to launch: 39 pages × 10 = 390 tokens per instrument (35,000 tokens,
70 minutes, for all 90). **A full tape of one instrument** is ≈ 29 requests a day
(BTC, ~2 trades/min) = 290 tokens a day. **Mark history at 1 s** is 14 million
points per instrument — use 1 m. Whole-account statistics without fills:
`/v1/info/pnl` and `/equity` (a few requests, ~15 tokens each) give the daily
realized series and equity curve since launch.

## 2. Variations weighed

- **Fills polling vs portfolio polling vs both** (Q7, Q14): fills are complete but
  cost 10 and lag ~1–1.5 s; the portfolio costs 5, is live and leads by ~1 s, but
  can be blind to net-zero windows and says nothing about prices or fees. A
  mixed scheme (portfolio to detect, fills to read, a slow fills safety net) is
  the cheapest that stays complete.
- **WebSocket instead of polling for following:** impossible (no account field).
  Useful only for prices and for "something traded on instrument X".
- **Reading history from the tape vs klines vs mark history** (Q10): tape for
  entry prices, klines for coarse ranges, mark for unrealized PnL.
- **Equity-minus-pnl vs `/v2/info/pnl` for deposits** (Q9): v2 is exact but covers
  18% of the accounts sampled; the v1 pair covers all with noise.
- **Leaderboard `address` row as a change trigger:** rejected — pod-snapshot flap.
- **Ceiling-test pace:** 2 a second, because a slow approach separates "refill"
  from "capacity" better than a flood; the first 429 came at read 53.
- **Fixed-minute budget vs bucket pacing:** the first would have allowed bursts
  that the second rejects; the poller must be a *spaced scheduler*.

## 3. Possibilities found

1. `/v1/info/pnl` + `/v1/info/equity` (public, undocumented in the spec): a whole
   account's daily/hourly realized PnL and equity curve since launch for ~25
   tokens — ROI, drawdown and flow estimates without reading fills.
2. `/v2/info/pnl` for new accounts: exact deposits and withdrawals (Q9).
3. **Counterparty pairs:** both sides of a trade share the trade_id, so two
   accounts' fills intersect by id — wash/self-trading and fixed market-maker
   counterparties can be found (R2).
4. Leaderboard rows carry `instruments_traded` and `top_instrument_ids`: a free
   breadth/focus signal for the style label.
5. `competition/account`: `disqualified`, boosted-volume share, unrealized PnL,
   fees and funding per account since 2026-11-02 in one weight-1 read.
6. WebSocket `bbo` / `trades` for live prices at alert time at no HTTP weight.
7. `top` of the day board has `pnl > 0` with `notional = 0` on 16 of 100 rows:
   accounts earning funding or from earlier fills only — a "holder" signal.
8. `leaderboard?address=` ranks any account beyond 1,000 — cheap standing for a
   favourite (but see the flap).
9. The undocumented `x-pmp-pod` header can separate snapshots per pod.

## 4. Potential problems, and how each would be handled

| Problem | Handling |
|---|---|
| Bucket burst ~90–100: ten fills reads at once → 429 | One scheduler per API client spacing requests ≥ weight/8.3 s (1.2 s for a fills read at 500/min), never a cron burst; jitter; on 429 back off ≥ 2 s and drop the tick, never retry in a loop; log the pod. |
| The owner's browser on polymarket.com/perps shares the bucket | Keep the poller at half the budget; on 429 pause 60 s. |
| Leaderboard snapshots differ by pod; `timestamp` can go back | Compare the max of the last 3 reads; hash the top 100; day board `total` must drop at 00:00Z. |
| IDs are random 53-bit | Zod `int().safe()`; key `(trade_id, instrument_id, timestamp)`; never order by id; do not assume uniqueness across the exchange. |
| Order within one millisecond is arbitrary | Order a poll batch by (timestamp, then event chain via `previous_size`); classify per event. |
| `previous_size` is per order event | Group fills by `(order_id, previous_size)`; the event's after-size is `prev + Σ`. A grouping bug misreads 11% of events. |
| The default fills window is 30 days | On first sight of a favourite, read from the start you need; afterwards `start_timestamp = last seen − 1 s`, dedupe by trade_id (start is inclusive). |
| Fills lag ~1–1.5 s and the cache 2 s | After a portfolio change wait ~2 s before the fills read. |
| Spec lags the live API; new fields appear | Zod objects non-strict (accept unknown fields); a failed parse is logged and dropped (Iron Rule 3). |
| Dormant accounts on `all` boards | Require recent fills (a 30-day fills read) before offering a favourite. |
| `/v2/info/pnl` is empty for old accounts | Show "flows unknown" for them; do not read an empty `data` as zero PnL. |
| The 429 reply is served by CloudFront (`x-cache: Error`) | Do not cache non-200s. |
| Weights of pnl/equity routes are unpublished | Charge 15 until measured. |
| Maintenance windows (docs: order placement pauses, WS drops) | Treat 5xx/503 as retry-after, pause the poller, resubscribe. |

## 6. Beyond the brief

- **Burst first:** the project's poller design depends on the bucket's burst, not
  on the per-minute figure. A node-cron tick that fires 50 favourites at once
  would be rejected on the 10th.
- **`GET /v1/info/position-snapshots` is a POST** that returns a fill's cycle id,
  size after, entry price and a chart; weight max(10, active + 7·history). Not
  called (not a GET; the brief). It could replace position-fills for share cards.
- The `volume` field of `all` boards uses ~1e9: float artefacts above ~$9B
  (changelog 2026-09); not relevant now.
- The `all` leaderboard grew from 34,877 ranked accounts (14:35Z) to 34,956
  (00:06Z next day, `out/lbmon.jsonl`): ~8 an hour, ~200 a day. The account-value
  census (8,192 accounts a 60 s tick) takes ~4.3 ticks for a full pass today.
  [Final review F6: "~60 accounts every 5 hours" was wrong.]
- Maker rebates (838 of 4,063 fills) and maker limit fills mean a follower
  cannot copy a maker fill's price; paper-follow must charge taker fees at the
  tape price after the delay. Roughly 44% of the corpus' fills were maker.
- Liquidation reduces are takers at off-market prices: do not alert on
  `liquidation: true` as a "decision".
- Time: the host is UTC+1; the server clock is +24 ms vs ours — fine.
- The docs describe an SDK (`@polymarket/client`) with typed Perps clients; it was
  not used (no installs), but its pagination helpers confirm the fields.
- Reading the account only needs the address; **an address can be case-folded**.
- Web app uses `assets.perpetuals.polymarket.com` for logos: no weight there.
