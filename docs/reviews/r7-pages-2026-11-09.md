from `date -u`). A design report: no code for the app, no app yet. Evidence:
the supervisor folder `workers/R7-PAGES/` (`progress.md`, the two small
scripts and their outputs) and the repository docs
named in each answer. API use: three public GET reads on 2026-11-09
20:21:30–36Z (tickers, leaderboard with `limit=3`, instruments; 9 weighted

## For the owner, in one page

This report designs the pages of v1. It is long because the plan subagent needs
every field; **you need only this page, Section 7 (the questions) and, for
pictures, the wireframes in Q1.** Addresses, labels and figures in the wireframes are invented.

**The eight pages, one line each**
1. **Watch** (the first screen): your favourites, what they hold and did since
   you last looked, and whether the app itself is healthy.
2. **Boards:** the exchange's 16 rankings (4 windows × 4 sorts) with each
   account's style label and flags where the app has looked; the rank is
   always the exchange's, never the app's.
3. **Trader:** one account: its label with the evidence, open positions, money
   banked against money still open, how it trades, a table of its positions.
4. **Alerts:** the permanent feed of what favourites did, with how late each
   alert was; the Windows toast says the same in three lines.
5. **Paper log:** the app's own forward test: would entering a minute after
   our alert beat random trades? It says "no verdict yet" until the evidence
   passes a bar fixed in advance.
6. **Status:** is the poller alive, how much of the rate budget is used, how
   fresh the data is, disk space, backups.
7. **Method:** a static reading guide: the labels, the statistics, the dated
   findings so far.
8. **Settings:** theme, time display, alert defaults.

**The four rules that matter most**
1. **Pages read only our own database.** A page that needs a fresh read puts a
   request in a queue the poller serves from its 50-token reserve; nothing
   else ever calls Polymarket (Q1, "The reserve's sum").
2. **Every number carries its base:** its count, its window and its age; a win
   rate only from 20 closed positions, with its range; ROI only beside the
   capital it is a percentage of; banked and open PnL side by side (Q3).
3. **A label is a block, not a colour:** class, window, coverage, reason,
   version; "not labelled yet" is different from "unclassified"; a directional
   label on thin evidence says so (Q2).
4. **Words and colour:** no "copy", "follow", "signal", "best", "score"; an
   evidence figure below its bar says "No verdict yet", never "no edge"; red
   means the app is broken or a position was liquidated, never "a trader lost
   money" (Q4, Q6).

**What v1 builds and what waits:** one table, "The cut-line", in Q1; waiting
items are marked **[later]**. **Ports:** API and built app 4300, development
server 3300, both free on 2026-11-09 (Q8). **What the record lacks:** Q8's last table.

**The 12 questions (Section 7), my recommendation first**
1. Ports 4300 (API + app) and 3300 (dev server)? Yes.
2. Times in UTC with the local time beside them? Yes.
3. ROI shown only above a $1,000 base (the record says "above $100")? Yes.
4. Red only for failures and liquidations? Yes.
5. Call the page "Paper log", with "follow" and "copy" off the screen? Yes.
6. "Thin evidence" = fewer than 8 decisions and fewer than 3 closed positions? Yes.
7. Let the poller label the boards you have viewed, in the background? Yes.
8. A day-clustered range in the paper log, only from 20 days? Yes.
9. Start the paper log with 20 trader slots even if you have fewer favourites? Yes.
10. A liquidation of any favourite always raises a banner, even an algorithm's? Yes.
11. A dismissible first-run note with three dated facts? Yes.
12. The board-turnover line and CSV export: later? Later.

**Where the sources were thin** (Section 5 and the evidence list): the research
on how readers misread error bars and dot displays was read at abstract and
summary level only, so the "soft band" is a proposal to try, not a finding.

## 1. The answers

### Q1. The pages, one by one

**Answer: eight pages — Watch (first screen), Boards, Trader, Alerts, Paper
log, Status, Method, Settings — that read the database only.** Each is set out
below with its purpose, its fields and their sources, the refresh and cost in
API tokens, the sort and filter controls, the loading / empty / stale / error
states, what it never shows, and a wireframe. Evidence: the routes and weights
(R1 Q1, external-apis), the flags and statistics (R2 Q5, Q9), alerts and silent
failure (R4 §5–§8), what is kept (R6 Q3), the paper entry and scoreboard (R3
Q6, Q7), the display defaults and the verdict (decisions 2026-11-08, 2026-11-09),
and Hyperdash, PMWallets and Polymarket's own pages read on 2026-11-09.

#### The rules every page obeys

1. **Pages read the database only.** The API process serves what the poller
   stored. A page never causes a Polymarket call by itself; "refresh" and
   "look at this account" write a request into a queue (`read_requests`:
   what, address, time asked, state, progress). The poller serves the queue
   from the 50-token reserve, never from the favourites' 200 (R1 Q2: one
   client, one request at a time, evenly spaced; decisions 2026-11-07, wave 1
   closed). Order inside the reserve: the nightly board copy, a request the
   owner just made, a board refresh, background labelling ("The reserve's
   sum", below). Costs are the poller's tokens, never the page's.
2. **Every block shows when it was true** ("as of 09:12Z, 40 s ago"). Past
   the block's limit it turns amber and says so in words; the numbers stay,
   muted. A block never goes blank without saying why.
3. **A rank is the exchange's.** Sorting by any column reorders rows and
   leaves the rank column as the board gave it; filters hide rows and say how
   many are hidden. (Hyperdash does the same, docs read 2026-11-09:
   leaderboard.md.) The app computes no rank, no score, no "best".
4. **Every address is a link** to its Trader page, shown as the owner's alias
   or `0x7a3f…c91e` (first 6, last 4), a copy icon beside it. A paste box in
   the header opens any address.
5. **Every ratio has its n on the same line**; a small n gets the "thin"
   style (dashed outline, muted number) and, for a win rate, no number at
   all below 20.
6. **Colour is never the only carrier** (WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.1,
   level A, w3.org, read 2026-11-09): each state has a word or a shape.
7. **Times are UTC with a Z** wherever a window or a delay is measured; the
   local time (UTC+1 now, UTC+2 from 2027-03-28) is beside it in the feed and
   in the toast. (Question 2 for the owner.)
8. **The five states of a block** (NN/g, Kate Kaplan, 2021-09-19, read
   2026-11-09: never leave a container blank, never show "no items" while
   loading):
   - **Loading:** a skeleton and the words for what is being read ("reading
     fills, page 2 of about 9").
   - **Empty:** says which kind — "nothing yet", "nothing matches your
     filters (31 hidden)", "not looked at yet" — and offers the next step.
   - **Stale:** amber word + age; data kept, muted.
   - **Error:** what failed, when it last worked, a retry; the last good data
     stays, greyed.
   - **Partial:** the blocks that loaded show; the others say "waiting for…".

**What costs API tokens, by route** (weights: external-apis, R1 Q1; "~15" is
R1's fit for the unpublished pnl/equity routes): leaderboard page of 100 rows
5 (1 if cached ≤ 5 s); portfolio 5; fills page of 100 rows 10; pnl, equity ~15
each; registered and competition reads 1; instruments and tickers 2; book at
depth 100, 5.

**The reserve's sum** (decisions 2026-11-07, wave 1 closed: 250 a minute in
all, 200 for the favourites, 50 in reserve for the leaderboard, tickers and
retries; arithmetic in `calc2.out.txt`). A favourite's poll and the detail read
of an alert belong to the 200 (R1 Q14). Everything else draws on the 50:

| **Standing total** | | **about 2–3** (1 at 5 favourites, 4–7 at 50) |

The owner's own requests may use up to 30 a minute; a board refresh comes
next; labelling takes only what is left, so all draws together stay under 45.
A page for an account the app has not looked at costs 52–82 tokens (the board
row, registered check, portfolio, 14 days of fills, competition row, pnl and
equity: 52 for a quiet account, 82 at R2's average): 2–3 minutes at the cap,
with a first paint of 16 tokens in about 30 s. Labelling the boards' tops costs
46 an account (about 40 for 14 days of fills — R2 Q6, a lower bound, heavy
accounts being capped — plus the portfolio 5 and the competition row 1): at most
400 accounts, 18,400 tokens, about 8 hours when the owner is away, 30 hours at
10 a minute while they are active.

**What is squeezed when the owner is active:** labelling first (to the 10 a
minute the cap leaves); board refreshes wait behind the owner's requests; two
account pages opened together take 4–6 minutes; from 23:50Z the nightly copy
takes the whole reserve for 8 minutes and the owner's requests wait. The
favourites are never squeezed, they have their own 200. **Not assumed:** lending
the favourites' unused share to the reserve (at 5 favourites R3 Q2's interval,
7.7 s, is what the 200 buys, so nothing is spare unless the plan sets a floor on
the poll interval). **Not in the record:** the cost of the freshness canary of
R4 §5 (one fills read a minute of one busy account would be 10 a minute, a fifth
of the reserve; the public trades WebSocket costs no tokens: R4 §5 detector 3);
and the owner's browser on polymarket.com spends the same bucket
(external-apis) with no rule, so the poller backs off on a 429.

#### Page 1 — Watch (the first screen, `/`)

**Purpose:** to show, in one screen, what the favourites hold and did since
the owner last looked, and whether the app itself is healthy.

**Data and refresh** (page reads every 10 s while the tab is visible, 0
tokens; the poller's own spend keeps the data fresh):

| Block / field | Source | Poller's refresh and cost |
|---|---|---|
| Health strip: newest fill seen, poller state, budget used, disk | `poller_state` heartbeat, `api_usage_*`, disk check | heartbeat each cycle; 0 extra |
| Since you last looked: the latest alerts | `alerts` | written when the poller sees an action |
| Favourite's alias, address | `favourites` | owner's input |
| Style chip with window, coverage, reason, version | `style_labels` | daily re-label over the trailing 14 days from stored fills, no extra tokens (R2 Q6) |
| Open positions count, exposure ÷ equity, equity | latest portfolio per favourite | portfolio poll 5 tokens every T seconds (T 7.7 s at 5 favourites, 33 s at 20, 100 s at 50: R3 Q2) |
| Banked (realized) and open (unrealized) PnL over 30 d, underwater flag | banked = the daily `/v1/info/pnl` buckets in `account_days` (realized after fees and funding; they sum to the board's `pnl` exactly: R1 Q9); open = latest portfolio | one `/pnl` read a day per favourite, ~15 tokens (unpublished weight) |
| Last action: kind, instrument, age | `alerts` | as above |
| Typical alert delay (median, 7 d) | `alerts` | derived |
| Polled every, mode (positions + fills on change / fills) | poller plan | derived |
| Paper log state: votes, E, "no verdict yet" | `paper_*` | derived from votes |
| Storage line **[later]**: fills a day, bytes a year | `fills` counts | derived (R6 §4 asks for it on heavy accounts) |

**Controls.** Tabs **Table** | **Positions by market [later]**. Sort by alias (default),
last action, equity, banked PnL, label; no default sort by profit (it would
make the favourites page a leaderboard of the owner's own picks; the app
stays out of ranking the owner's own picks). Filters: label class, "has an open
position", "alerts off/digest". **+ Add by address** (paste; the app checks
`registered`, 1 token, and says "not an account on Polymarket Perps" when
false). **Remove from favourites** (the star on the Trader page or a row
menu) says what happens: "Alerts and the paper log stop; open paper entries
are marked at this instant; the stored fills stay." Alias and a free-text
note are edited in place on the Trader page header.

**Positions by market [later]:** a table of instruments held by at least one
favourite: instrument, how many favourites long and short, total long and
short notional, and the favourites' alias chips. Counts and sums only; it
never says "consensus", "most are long" or what to do. Source: the latest
portfolio rows; 0 tokens.

**Empty (no favourites yet):** "Nothing is watched yet. Look at the boards
(Day, Week, Month, All) or paste an address." Plus the first-run note: three
dated facts (Section 6, item 4): the boards' tops are
mostly algorithms, market makers and inactive accounts (R2, 2026-11-08);
entering the top 50 directional traders' new positions about 80 s after them
lost money and was no better than random trades (R8, 2026-11-09); every figure here is a
description, never advice.

**Loading / stale / error:** the health strip loads first. A row whose
portfolio is older than 3 × its poll interval (or 2 minutes, whichever is
longer) gets "stale 4 min" in amber and its numbers mute. If the API process
itself is down the browser shows "The app server is not answering (last good
09:14Z)" with the last cached screen.

**Never shows:** a ranking of favourites by performance, a composite score,
"projected" PnL, a leverage figure (the API has none: external-apis
2026-11-06; the table says "exposure ÷ equity"), a liquidation price, a
buy/sell button or any control that moves money.

#### Page 2 — Boards (`/boards`)

**Purpose:** to browse the exchange's public rankings with what the app
knows about each account, without letting a rank pass for a recommendation.

**The boards the API offers** (external-apis 2026-11-06, R1 Q3): `window` day
| week | month | all (UTC calendar windows: day from 00:00Z, week from Monday
00:00Z, month from the 1st, all from launch) × `sort_by` pnl | notional |
account_value | roi = **16 boards**, each the top 1,000 at most (100 a page,
`offset + limit ≤ 1000`); any account's own row and rank, however far down,
through `address`. Plus the **Competition** board while one is live
(`competition/leaderboard`, weight 1; 2026-11-02 14:30Z – 2026-11-16 14:30Z,
77 ranked on 2026-11-07). Controls: a window switch (Day, Week, Month, All),
a sort switch (PnL, Volume (notional), Account value, ROI), a Competition
tab **[later]**; the window text reads "Week: since Monday 00:00Z" so the owner never
mistakes it for a trailing seven days.

**Fields per row and their source:**

| Column | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Rank | board row `rank` | fixed; sorting never changes it |
| Trader | `account` | alias or short address, link |
| Style chip + flags | `style_labels`, portfolio, competition row | "not labelled yet" when the app has not looked; flags below |
| Account value | board `account_value` | |
| PnL (window) | board `pnl` = realized − fees + funding, unrealized excluded (exact: R1) | column header says so |
| Open PnL now | latest portfolio, only for accounts the app has looked at | "as of" age; "—" otherwise |
| Volume | board `notional` (Σ price × quantity of fills: R2 §8) | |
| ROI and its base | board `roi`; base = `pnl ÷ roi` (derived, not an API field) | shown as "2.0% on about $2.47M"; below a display floor "—" |
| Instruments | `instruments_traded`, `top_instrument_ids` → symbols | names from `instruments` |
| Last fill | stored fills or the fills read | greys the row when none in 30 days |

**Flags beside the label** (R2 Q5; decisions 2026-11-08): underwater (needs
the portfolio), dormant (no fill in the 14-day label window: the flag of the
definitions, R2 Q5) and greyed (no fill in 30 days: decisions 2026-11-08; greyed,
not hidden), tiny or
emptied (account value under $100), competition (live/qualified/disqualified:
a flag, never a label), liquidated in the window, and "board standing" is
inside the Trader page.

**Refresh and cost.** The page opens on the stored snapshot, shows its age,
and when that is older than 5 minutes asks the poller to re-read the visible
board's top 100 (5 tokens; deeper pages 5 each; the whole top 1,000 is 50).
The daily final copy of 8 boards × 1,000 (400 tokens, started 23:50Z, day
boards last) is the history (R6 Q3, decisions 2026-11-08). "Yesterday's
final" is a free switch on every window. Labelling the boards' tops: Beyond
the brief 1.

**Sort and filter.** Sort: any numeric column; the rank column stays. Filters:
style class (D, A, M, C, U, not labelled yet), "hide greyed rows" (default off), minimum account value, instrument category (crypto, equity, index,
commodity, pre-IPO), "in the live competition". **Every filter prints what it
hides:** "Directional: 14 shown · 61 not directional · 25 not labelled yet".

**Standing captions** (selection bias stated where it misleads), under the
board's title, one line each:
- PnL boards: "Ranked on the past. In our own check (six weeks to 2026-11-02,
  weekly boards rebuilt from fills inside R2's pool of leaderboard accounts)
  only 18–32 of each week's top 50 were positive the next week. [How we read this]" (R2 Q9, 2026-11-08).
- Volume boards: "Volume ranks activity, not results; the live competition
  pays for volume." (R5 Q3: competitions score PnL × √boosted volume; R2 Q7:
  67 of the all-time volume board's top 100 were non-directional.)
- ROI board: "On this board small accounts rank high; ROI below a base of
  $1,000 is not shown." The $1,000 floor is a proposal (question 3): the record
  says "a display floor above $100" (decisions 2026-11-07, (4)) and
  Polymarket's own base floor is $100 (external-apis).
- Day board before 12:00Z: "This board covers 5 h 12 min of today only"
  (R2 §6: it is meaningless early in the UTC day; 'Yesterday's final' beside it).

**States.** Loading: the stored rows show at once, the refresh runs behind
them. Empty filtered: "No account in this top 100 matches (use 'Load next
100')". Stale: snapshot older than 5 min on open triggers the refresh; older
than 24 h with no refresh possible: amber "from 2026-11-07". Error: the 429 or
a failed read shows "Polymarket asked us to wait (retry in 2 s)" and the
stored rows stay. The API's pods serve different snapshots (`timestamp` can
go back 67 s, R1 Q3), so each page shows its own `timestamp` and the page
never replaces a newer snapshot with an older one.

**Never shows:** the board re-ranked by anything the app computed; a "top
traders" or "best" heading (the heading is the board's own: "Week, by PnL");
ROI without its base; a profile photo or a name the API did not give.

#### Page 3 — Trader (`/traders/:address`)

**Purpose:** to let the owner see, for one account, who it looks like (the
label with its evidence), what it holds, what it did, what it has banked and
how sure each statement is.

The same page serves a favourite and any other registered account. For an
account the app has not looked at, the page opens with the **board row** (0
tokens if the row was in a stored snapshot; otherwise one `address` read, 5)
and then loads in this order, each block appearing as it arrives: (1) the
portfolio (5), (2) the label, from 14 days of fills read page 1 first (10; about
40 in all for a typical account: R2 Q6, a lower bound), (3) the competition row
(1), (4) the pnl and equity history (30). Total 52–82 tokens with the board row
and the registered check: a first paint (16 tokens) in about 30 s, the whole
page in 2–3 minutes at the owner's cap (see "The reserve's sum"); a progress
line says so. A favourite has all of
this stored and the page costs nothing.

**Header:** alias (editable), address (copy), first fill seen, registered,

#### Page 4 — Alerts (`/alerts`)

**Purpose:** the complete, permanent record of what each favourite did as the
poller saw it, with how late each alert was, so a dismissed or missed toast can
always be found.

**Fields** (source: `alerts`, written by the poller before the toast is shown,
R4 §6 and R6 Q3; about 15 MB a year at 20 typical favourites): time (UTC, local
beside it) · trader (alias, chip) · kind · the three text lines of the toast
(R4 §7) · fills in the episode and first/last fill time · the delay line (fill
time → seen → shown) · flags (late, data stale, outage) · seen/unseen · a link
to the Trader page opened on this alert. **Kinds:** open, add, reduce, close,
flip, liquidation; hourly digest; late summary; system (poller silent, data
stale, label changed, notifications off, disk warning). **Refresh:** the header
asks for new alerts every 5 s while the tab is visible; the table every 10 s;
0 tokens. The poller's spend on an alert is the fills read that gives its
detail (10, after a ~2 s wait past the cache, R1 Q7).

**Grouping on screen** (R4 §6, decisions 2026-11-07): open, close, flip and
liquidation are one row each, at once. Adds and reduces by the same trader on
the same instrument and direction wait for a quiet window (20 s, never more
than 60 s from the first fill) and become one row ("added to ETH long: +1.20
ETH ($3,170) in 6 fills over 12 s, now 1.70 ETH"), expandable to its fills.
An episode is grouped by `order_id` and the `previous_size` chain, not fill by
fill (code-rules, "Read fills per order event"). **Digest rows:** favourites
labelled algorithm or market maker produce one row an hour — fills, decisions,
instruments touched, net change per instrument, banked PnL in the hour — and
no banner; their fills are still stored (decisions 2026-11-08, R6 accepted).
The digest row is styled quieter and collapsed by default.

**Late alerts, three kinds, never confused:**
1. *A slow alert:* a normal alert whose measured delay is long (over 2 minutes)
   — still one row, amber chip "seen 3 m 10 s after the fill".
2. *A catch-up after a gap* (the PC slept or restarted; the gap exceeded about
   two poll intervals or the fill is over 5 minutes old, R4 §8): **no per-fill
   rows as fresh alerts**; one row "LATE · caught up 38 min of fills, 30 fills,
   4 traders, 13:10Z–13:48Z (PC was off)" that expands to the fills, each marked
   "late by n min", plus the positions each trader holds now.
3. *Data stale* (the exchange's data did not move, R4 §5 failure 2): rows
   written during the state carry a "data stale" chip.

**The delay shown honestly.** Each row shows three stamps and two differences:
`fill 14:03:12Z → seen 14:03:59Z (+47 s) → shown +0.4 s`. The page header
carries the running figures: "Last 7 days: 212 alerts, median delay 21 s, 90th
percentile 48 s, slowest 96 s, 3 late." A poll can never see a fill sooner than
the next poll, so no row can be faster than the poll interval allows; the Watch
table prints each favourite's typical delay beside its polling interval.

**Sort and filter.** Time, newest first (oldest first as a switch). Filters:
trader (several), kind group (actions | digests | system), label class,
date range, only unseen, only late, minimum notional. "Mark all seen" is a
button; **seen means the feed was opened or the button pressed, never that a
toast was dismissed** (R4 §5 failure 4: the OS gives a PowerShell notifier no
dismissal signal).

**States.** Empty: "No actions yet. 8 favourites are being watched; last check
4 s ago." Poller silent: a red banner "Poller silent since 21:12Z — nothing new
can arrive" over the existing rows. Data stale: amber banner with the since
time. Error: "The feed could not be read" with the last page kept.

**Never shows:** the words "buy", "sell" (as an instruction), "should",
"consider", "signal", "opportunity", "copy" (R4 §7); a size relative to what
"you" would trade; a trader's strength or track record as a headline; any
button that would act on an alert.

### Q3. Statistics that stay honest on screen

**Answer: a short grammar that every number follows, plus three components
(a rate, a range, an evidence readout) that make a small or uncertain figure
look small or uncertain.**

**The grammar: `value · range or base · n · window · as of`.** The pieces
that do not apply are dropped; none of the pieces that do apply may be.

| Statistic | The rule (source) | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| Banked and open PnL | the board counts closed trades only (R2 Q5; decisions 2026-11-06, the pre-test and waves 2–4 approved, lesson (4)) | two numbers side by side, labelled "Banked (closed, after fees and funding)" and "Open (unrealized)", then "Together"; never one without the other where the portfolio is known |
| Underwater | banked > 0 while banked + open < 0 (55 accounts of 1,615; the PnL board's highest-ranked directional account had $179,336 banked and −$280,702 open: R2 Q5) | a chip `UNDERWATER` plus the sentence "Banked +$179k; open positions −$281k. The board counts only the banked part." Another 100 accounts had open losses above half their banked profit: chip `OPEN LOSS > ½ BANKED` (quieter) |
| ROI | only beside its base and the board window it belongs to (the API gives day, week, month and all, nothing else); display floor above $100 (decisions 2026-11-07, (4)); Polymarket's own base is the larger of $100 and starting equity + peak net deposits (external-apis) | "2.0% on a base of about $2.47M"; the base is `pnl ÷ roi` from the board row (checked on three rows 2026-11-09 20:21Z, `calc1.out.txt`: 50,563.60 ÷ 0.020435 = $2.474M against account value $2.493M); rounding in `roi` (6 decimals) limits it to about 0.05% when \|roi\| ≥ 0.001; below the floor (proposed $1,000 as Hyperdash, R5 Q3) "—, base under $1,000" |
| Win rate | only from 20 closed positions, with its interval and shrinkage (decisions 2026-11-08); per position cycle, never per fill; closed positions only (Binance's way, not Bybit's open-winner count: R5 Q3) | below 20: "11 closed positions: 7 won, 4 lost — a rate is shown from 20". From 20: "65% won (13 of 20) · 95% range 43–82% · pulled toward the typical trader: 61%". Always with "average win $190 · average loss $240" beside it, because a win rate says nothing about whether money was made |
| Holding time | per closed position; the median with quartiles and the count | "median 6.9 h (p25 1.2 h – p75 26 h) · n 11 · thin" |
| Drawdown | never from equity alone (deposits and withdrawals move it: R2 Q9) | from the total-PnL series where `/v2/info/pnl` answers; else "drawdown of banked PnL" named as such |
| Counts of activity | decisions, closed positions, orders, fills are different things (R2 Q1: read per decision, never per fill) | the word "trades" is not used; the table header names the unit |
| Open exposure | the API has no leverage field (external-apis) | "exposure ÷ equity 1.9×", never "leverage" |
| Largest gain | a robustness line | "banked without its largest gain: +$1,180" |

**Showing an uncertain number so it reads right at a glance** (the design
choices, with what supports them):
1. **Say it in counts first.** "7 of 11 won" before "64%", and the vote strip
   shows each vote as a dot. This is my design choice. One study points the
   same way — dot displays of 50 outcomes beat a text-only interval in a
   bus-timing task (Fernandes, Walls, Munson, Hullman, Kay, CHI 2018) — but I
   saw only a search-result summary of it; the paper was not read:
   **unverified.**
2. **Print both ends of a range as numbers and draw the range plainly.** The
   authors of "Error Bars Considered Harmful" (Correll and Gleicher, IEEE TVCG
   2014) report on their page that viewers misread error bars even on basic
   judgments (abstract, read 2026-11-09). That readers treat the bar as a
   container, and that gradient and violin plots read better, I have only from
   a search summary; the paper could not be read here: **unverified.** So the
   range is a plain band with both ends printed; a soft-edged band (solid at
   the estimate, fading to the ends) is a proposal to try on the owner
   (Section 5), not a finding. Caption: "a range, not a promise".
3. **Draw the reference line that means "nothing going on"**: 50% on a win
   rate, zero on a return, the bar on an E. The eye then asks "does the band
   cross the line?" and the printed words answer it ("the range includes
   50%").
4. **A small n looks small.** Under the minimum a rate is not printed. Above it
   but small (20–49 closed positions) the band is wide and the n is printed in
   the same line. For averages and medians below 20 observations the number
   is shown muted with a "thin" tag and its n.
5. **Two numbers that must be read together are kept in one cell** (banked +
   open; win rate + average win/loss; E + matched E).
6. **No red/green for good/bad of an estimate** (Section 6, colour rule).

**The paper log's evidence readout** is the component in Page 5: the vote strip
(dots), the E track on a log scale with the bar as a line, and the sentence.
Its states:

| State | Condition | The words |
|---|---|---|
| No votes | n = 0 | "No votes yet." |
| No verdict yet | E below the bar | "No verdict yet — it needs E of 400 (20 trader slots; 20 for the pooled board). You may look as often as you like; the bar already allows for that." |
| Verdict, better | E ≥ bar and more votes better than worse | "Verdict: better than random trades. This is about the entries in this log under its fixed rules, not a promise about the next one." |
| Verdict, worse | E ≥ bar and more worse | "Verdict: worse than random trades." (same second sentence) |

The *matched-direction* figure sits **beside** every E (decisions 2026-11-09,
the owner's ruling (2)): "Against random trades that go the way of this
trader's habit (long 64% of entries): E 0.5". The sentence under it: "If this is
much lower than the figure beside it, the trader's long habit in a rising
market, not an advantage, explains the difference" (R3 Q6 point 8). The *day
clustering*: the pooled board counts one vote a day by construction; the
per-trader page prints "42 votes on 27 distinct days", and the secondary
panel's range is clustered by day (and by trader on the pooled board) with
"too few clusters (n; 20 needed) to give a range" below 20 (a proposal: R8's
TOO FEW line was 20 traders with 5+ entries; the live log reuses the number,
question 8).

**Selection bias stated where it misleads** (each is one line, dated):
- Boards (caption, Page 2): the persistence figure (R2 Q9, 2026-11-08) and
  "ranked on the past".
- Trader page, Board standing: "Where an account stood on a board describes
  the past; this app counts an account only from the day it is added."
- Add-from-board dialog: the same sentence, with the rank and board named.
- Paper log: "Only entries made after an account was added count; no entry
  before." and the whole-set rule (no hand-picked subsets, Iron Rule 4).
- Method: the earlier test's finding (R8, 2026-11-09) and what is still open
  (B2's confirmation needs weeks 9–12: decisions 2026-11-09).

**Light and dark.** Follow Windows through `prefers-color-scheme` (baseline
since January 2020: MDN, read 2026-11-09), with a manual override in Settings;
colours as tokens (a few neutrals, one accent, an amber for caution, a red for
failure), contrast checked in both modes. **The colour rule: red means the app
is broken or a position was liquidated; it never means a trader lost money.**
A loss is a minus sign in the normal text colour (and a slightly muted tint
that is not red). Why: red and green on PnL read as good/bad, which pulls a
reader toward chasing and away from describing (Iron Rule 6), and red and green are
hard to tell apart in the commonest kinds of colour-blindness (general
knowledge, not looked up here); sign and word carry the meaning (WCAG 1.4.1).

first-run note (Page 1).

change favourites or settings through the API; the machine's MongoDB has no
authentication and also listens only on 127.0.0.1: R6 Q4).

1. **Labelling the boards is the work that makes the Boards page worth

   The cost is real (18,400 tokens once for the four PnL boards' top 100:
   see "The reserve's sum") and has to be a visible job on Status
   ("labelled 212 of 340"), at the lowest priority and with its own cap (it
   shares the 50-token reserve with the owner's requests and the boards'
   refresh), so it never delays a favourite.
2. **The cut-line** is a single table in Q1, after the pages at a glance.
3. **The tab title is a status light** that works when the window is behind
   another: `(3) Alerts` normally, `⚠ poller silent` when not.
4. **A first-run note of three dated facts**, dismissible and kept in Method:
   the boards' tops are mostly algorithms, market makers and inactive accounts
   (R2, 2026-11-08: 67 of the all-time notional top 100 non-directional, the
   first directional at #47); entering the top 50 directional traders' new
   positions about 80 s after them lost 16.6 bps an entry and was no better
   than random trades (R8, 2026-11-09); and no figure in the app is advice. These are findings, dated,
   not instructions (Iron Rule 6), and they answer the question the owner
   asked of this tool ("who is a real trader and who is an algorithm") before
   the first board is opened.
5. **"Who is a real trader" cannot be answered, and the app should say so
   once.** A label is what the activity looks like (a person who clicks a
   preset size reads as an algorithm: R2 §4). Method says it in one line; the
   label block repeats "does not say who is behind the account".
6. **The owner's aliases are the one owner-written, non-re-readable data.**
   The weekly test restore (R6) should check `favourites` by name.
7. **How we watch this account**, honestly, in the Trader header: positions
   every 17 s plus fills on a change, or fills only, chosen by the label (R1
   Q7, decisions 2026-11-07), with the miss rate that mode has (0–1.9% for
   slow accounts, up to 6.8% for a scalper) — the poller's blind spot is part
   of the data's quality and should not be a surprise.
8. **A page must state what its numbers exclude.** The Boards PnL excludes
   unrealized PnL and, most likely, liquidity rewards (R2 Q2: not proven); a
   market maker's board PnL is not its income. One sentence beside a maker's
   PnL ("liquidity rewards are not counted here") does it.
9. **Keyboard and contrast are part of "simple".** `j`/`k`/Enter, visible
   focus, a text equivalent for every chart (its caption), contrast checked
   in both modes. Cheap now, costly to retrofit.
10. **The competition is a regime on the Boards [later].** While one is live, a chip
    says so with its dates (`competitions`, 1 token): volume is rewarded, which
    can make accounts trade like algorithms (R5 §6 item 3); the Competition
    board is a tab and `row_state` a flag.
