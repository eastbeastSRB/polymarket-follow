## 1. The answers

### The recommendation for R8: `session_pair`

*My table at instants in US regular hours (Mon–Fri 09:30–16:00 New York), R3's table at every
other instant, for all instruments.* In plain words:

- **The two tables describe two different markets.** R3's was taken 2026-11-07 00:53–05:21Z. In
  New York that is **Friday 19:53–00:21**, after the US extended session: equities, indices and
  commodities shut, only crypto live (97.4% of its window lies in the weekend closure by the usual
  US schedule, an assumption the docs do not state). It is not "a Saturday". Mine is the opening
  two hours of the US session. (`out/clock-shares.txt`)
- **By the clock R8's six weeks (2026-09-26 → 2026-11-07, DST ending 2026-11-01 taken into
  account) are 19.3% US regular hours, 28.7% weekend closure and 52.0% other weekday hours**
  (weekday evenings, nights, pre- and after-market). I did not read when R8's entries happen; the
  supervisor can count entry *times* (not results) and set the share in `choices.txt`.
- `session_pair` is exact where a table was measured — regular hours (mine) and the weekend
  closure (R3's), **48% of the clock** — and in the other 52% it equals `saturday`, because
  nobody has measured those hours. It is therefore **never farther from the truth than
  `saturday`**, and closer than `weekday` unless the unmeasured hours cost at least 78% as much
  extra as the weekday morning does (`out/choices.txt`).
- **What each would get wrong** (per side at $1,000, bps; "understates" = following looks cheaper
  than the book offered, the error to avoid). D is the weekday morning's cost minus R3's, median
  over a category's instruments of half-spread + extra $1,000 (`out/choices.txt`):

- **How big is the choice?** Small outside ANT. At the clock shares the difference between

  against 8 bps of fees; if R8's entries cluster in US hours (R share 0.40–0.60) it is 0.5–0.8.
  For ANT (secondary cell) it is 1.9. The things that move R8's cost more are
  *instrument-specific* (below: PRL-USD, NCLD-USD, SKHYNIX/SAMSUNG/CXMT, ANT).
- **If the owner wants the table that cannot make following look cheaper in any hour measured
  (outside ANT),** that is `weekday`: it is right in the morning and too expensive only on the
  weekend. It buys that safety by overstating about half the clock (the weekend closure and, if

- **The opening half hour is the widest.** My table is open-weighted: the 14:30–15:00Z half hour

## 2. Variations weighed

1. **The sampler in the window:** R3's Session A (bbo for all 90, then four depth-100 books per
   ~29 s) against Session B (two books and two tape pages per ~43 s). I ran A for the whole window
   (twice the books) and read the tape *after* 16:30Z with `tapeonly.mjs` (Session B's `readTape`
   unchanged): the tape is history, so it cost the window nothing.
2. **The tape on the WebSocket** (`trades::<iid>`, no HTTP weight) against the HTTP tape. The
   brief says "the same channels" and "at most a few subscriptions"; 28 trade channels is not a
   few. HTTP, 108 reads of ~10 tokens.
3. **How long to read the tape.** `an-q1b` skips an instant after the tape's last trade, so a tape
   ending at 16:28Z would under-count slow instruments' long gaps at the end. Two passes: tape to
   ~16:50–17:09Z by instrument.
4. **Pacing.** R3's lib paced by the previous call's weight and went above 50 in a trailing minute
   at 53 of 1,796 calls (max 54). I added admission (trailing 60.5 s + weight ≤ 50, trailing 5.5 s + weight ≤
   30) to `lib.mjs`, tested on a virtual clock first. Result: max 50 in any 60 s, 0 of 1,366 calls
   above 50, max 15 in 10 s (`out/an-budget.txt`).
5. **Mean or median in the table:** the mean, as R3 (a follower at a random instant pays the mean);
   the median is in the same file because two instruments show how far a mean can be pulled.
6. **One table or two:** the full window as the deliverable; a 15:00Z-onward table beside it
   as a sensitivity (the open's weight).
7. **Recommendation:** the three of R8's rules, plus the "conservative envelope" (the higher of the
   two tables per instrument), which R8's rules do not define: not recommended as the primary
   (it would overstate roughly half the clock), but it is what a never-cheaper table looks like.

## 3. Possibilities found

1. **An hour-of-week cost table is cheap:** the bbo of all 90 instruments is weight 2; one snapshot
   a minute for a week is 2 tokens a minute (0.8% of the 250 budget) and gives hour × category
   spreads for nights, afternoons and Sundays, settling the 52% nobody measured.
2. **R8's mark rule works for its period:** 1 s mark buckets exist back to at least 2026-09-28.
3. **On weekdays the tape is dense** (BTC 1,351, NAS100 1,316 and NVDA 772 trades in the tape,
   14:30 to ~16:50–17:09Z), so trade-anchored checks of the mark are possible but not better than
   the mark.
4. **The forward app can log its own spread for free:** `bbo::<iid>` on the WebSocket costs no HTTP
   weight (not used here: the brief allows a few subscriptions).
5. **Session state is visible in the data:** the share of seconds with mark = index and the
   book − index premium change with the session (ANT's premium flipping at 16:00Z), so a
   session flag could be inferred where the docs give no hours.
