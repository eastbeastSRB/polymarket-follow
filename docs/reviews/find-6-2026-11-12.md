  the percentile ranks, among the eligible, of the profit factor of each

  streak, over the cycles closed in the formation and those still open at e

- **The groups** (R6): k = round(candidates ÷ 5) for every rule (21 and 19);

- **The race** (R7): 100,000 permutations (seed 20261112) give each active
  candidate the votes of another of the same stratum (active in window 1
  only, window 2 only, both; a trader's two windows together), every top
  fifth kept as formed; a rule's adjusted p = (1 + the permutations whose

  have both estimates > 0 and a larger p < 0.05. The verdict: PASSES if a rule
  other than rule 4 passes; else "competition only"; else "entry 1's
  persistence again" if rule 4 alone passes; else FAILS.
- **After a finding** (R11), for the winning rule only: instants moved by a
  day, 1,000 random-trade draws, leave-one-out. **The register** (R12): only on
  a pass.

## 6. Potential problems

1. **The race could see only large differences** (section 4). This "no" says
   little against a real difference under 0.4 risk units — and FIND-1's
   estimate puts any real spread well under it. The main limit, stated on the
   owner's page.
2. **Rule 7's day test alone was under 0.05** (0.044; without the
   competition's days 0.040). It is not a near pass: A.0-6 lets the larger of
   the two p decide (its trader test, p 0.244, has the largest error of the
   seven, 0.222 on 23 top traders), and the race pays for choosing among
   seven (adjusted 0.55; a pass needed a deciding p near 0.04). Its
   entry-weighted mean d is −0.100 for the top, against −0.007 for the rest:
   the top's busiest traders did worse than its others.
3. **Per trader and per copy disagree.** The follower's top − rest by trader
   and by copy differ by up to 81 bps and, for rules 1, 2, 6 and both of rule
   7's prints, in sign: a few busy traders carry the per-copy means. Both are
   printed; neither is tested beyond the Welch line.
4. **The seven rows are not seven tries.** Rules 1, 2, 3 and 5 read profit and
   share traders: the same −8,855.9 bps run sits in four of their top groups.
   The race pays for this correlation exactly (R7); a reader of the table
   should not count seven independent chances.
5. **The follower's net is not d.** d removes the market's own move in the
   copy's hour and weighs markets by their usual move; the net is the money,
   costs and drift included, in raw bps (wild markets weigh more). So rule 7's
   top can be ahead in d (+0.16 / +0.27) and behind in net (−53 against −3
   bps). Both are within chance.
6. **The competition** fills the last 4.4 days of window 2's measure (383 of
   its 883 copies). The strict lines are printed; no rule reached them.
7. **Rule 7's groups are small**: 12 and 13 of its 21 and 19 top accounts made

   traders and 393 copies, and its fifth of the eligible on 9 traders and 120
   copies — too few for any conclusion. An account that makes no copy adds
   nothing (R6); it makes groups smaller, not better.
