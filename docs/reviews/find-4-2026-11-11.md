## For the owner, in one page

**The question.** Even if no trader is reliably better than the others, some
*kinds* of entries might be: entries taken right after a sudden price jump,
entries unusually large for the trader, entries against the trader's usual
side, and so on. We tested eleven such kinds, written down and sealed before
any result was seen.

**What we measured.** For every entry, the price move over the 24 hours after
a follower could have copied it (about 1 to 1.5 minutes late), minus what a
random trade in the same market and the same hour would have made. Then, for
each kind, whether its entries did better or worse than the others, comparing
like with like (the same type of market, the same four hours of the day).
The data: 7,529 entries of 367 traders on 67 days (2026-08-31 to 11-05), and
3,974 additions to open positions for one of the kinds.

**The answer: no kind of entry stood out.** None of the eleven passed the
line set for testing eleven at once. The closest was "after a jump": entries
placed just after the price moved more than three times its usual 15-minute
move did *worse*, by about 50 bps, than entries placed in calmer minutes. It
still did not pass — the line asks that a difference this large turn up by
luck less than 0.45% of the time with no real effect, and this one would turn
up 0.82% of the time. No kind is shown to earn more than the others.

[Final review, FIND-REVIEW-A, F1 (`find-review-a-2026-11-11.md`, M1): the
end of this paragraph is withdrawn. Was: "— and a second check stopped it
outright: **random trades in the same hours, when they too came just after a
jump, did worse as well.** So whatever it is belongs to the market after a
jump (or to how the measure compares a trade late in a fast hour with that
hour's other trades), not to the traders. Following entries of any of these
kinds would not have earned more than following the others." That second
check used one random trade for each entry, its side decided by a coin.
Drawn again 1,000 times, the same check shows nothing on average (−0.005 and
−0.004 of a usual day's move) and comes out as far below zero as that one
draw in 1 to 3 draws of 1,000: the repeat was the luck of one draw, not
something random trades do. Nor is it the hour: measured against the 15
minutes, or the single minute, around each random trade, the result is the
same. So the look does not say whose pattern this is. What stands: "after a
jump" missed the line, so it is no finding; it is the one near miss of the
eleven, and only weeks nobody has read can test it.]

**What it means.** Nothing from entry 4 goes to the exams, so it takes none
of the batch's places. Entries 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 and 10 also failed their FIND
today (the supervisor's word); entry 4 adds no pattern a follower could use.
That is an honest "no": it saves you from trusting something that would cost
money.

**How sure.** The test could see a difference of about 50–95 bps between the
two halves of the larger kinds (0.25–0.40 of a usual day's move). A smaller
real difference, say 10–20 bps, could exist and stay unseen here; six weeks of
exams would not see it either (section 4).
